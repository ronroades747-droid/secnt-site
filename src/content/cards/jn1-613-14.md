Most of us hear John 1:11, "his own received him not," as something that happens inside a person: an idea not accepted, a decision not made.

But John pictures someone arriving at a house. Herman Ridderbos puts it plainly: his own were "unwilling to receive him as a welcome guest."

And the one at the door was no guest. He came to what was his own, the world he had made, and his own people did not welcome him. Calvin saw what that meant for Israel: "Christ therefore was first offered to them as his own household, and as belonging to his empire by a peculiar right."

But it is not the last word. The very next verse turns to those who did take him in, and gave them the right to become children of God.
