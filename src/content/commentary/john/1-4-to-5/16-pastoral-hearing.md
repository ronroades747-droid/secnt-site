The exegesis of the unit is complete, and the doctrine developed on it. What remains is to hear these two verses as those who will preach and teach them must hear them, and to name the trajectory they open into the rest of the Prologue and into the Gospel. Neither task re-exegetes; both gather. And what is gathered already has a shape: the last service this commentary can do for the unit is to hand it over in the form the evangelist gave it.

## The pastoral hearing

The unit is composed, and it preaches as it is composed. [Its four clauses climb a staircase](/commentary/john/1-4-to-5/02-gradatio-and-foyer/), each taking up the last weighted word of the one before it, and the teacher can walk a congregation up the same steps the evangelist built — one arc in four movements, in the order the text itself sets them. The audience of this work is the minister and the teacher, and the pastoral hearing of John 1:4–5 is not a softening of its scholarship into reassurance. It is a naming of what the one who preaches these verses is given to preach, and every step of it is a thing the text’s own grammar, not the teacher’s piety, puts there.

It begins where the gospel begins — not with the hearer’s need, but with God. [ἐν αὐτῷ ζωὴ ἦν](/commentary/john/1-4-to-5/06-ontology-life-in-him/): *in him was life.* Before anything at all is said about darkness, or about seeing, or about being saved, the ground is laid. The life of every person a preacher will ever address is, at this moment, a life held in the One through whom all things were made — not merely given by him once and then let go, but [held in him now](/commentary/john/1-4-to-5/10-life-in-the-word/), as a standing that has never been anywhere else. That is not first a summons. It is first a fact about the hearer, true before he ever considered it, and it is where the preaching of these verses has to start.

From that ground the movement turns toward the hearer. [ἡ ζωὴ ἦν τὸ φῶς τῶν ἀνθρώπων](/commentary/john/1-4-to-5/07-anthropology-light-of-men/): the life was the light of men. This is not a distant deity, high and self-contained, whose existence the hearer is asked to concede. It is the light by which the human creature was made to see and to live — humanity’s own light, the [dignity written into being made in God’s image](/commentary/john/1-4-to-5/11-image-and-light-of-men/). The turn matters pastorally because it is the one the hearer does not expect: the eternal life of the Word is not held away from him as a fact about God alone, but named, in the same breath, as the very thing he was made for.

Then the movement turns to the hearer’s actual condition, and it does so without flattery. [τὸ φῶς ἐν τῇ σκοτίᾳ φαίνει](/commentary/john/1-4-to-5/08-cosmology-light-in-darkness/): the light shines in the darkness. The darkness is named honestly — the moral and mental dark of a race turned from its Maker, real, and still dark. The preposition is the mercy in the sentence: the light does not shine *at* the darkness from a safe distance, but *within* it, having entered the very condition of those who do not know it. [The dark is real and it is not ultimate](/commentary/john/1-4-to-5/12-light-within-darkness/), and both halves of that have to be preached together — a hearer told only the first despairs, and a hearer told only the second has not been told the truth about his own life.

And it ends on assurance. [ἡ σκοτία αὐτὸ οὐ κατέλαβεν](/commentary/john/1-4-to-5/09-soteriology-not-overcome/): the darkness did not overcome it. The word carries both its senses at once, and the fallen condition fails at both — it can neither take the light in and understand it, nor take hold of the light and put it out. [What the darkness could not do](/commentary/john/1-4-to-5/13-darkness-cannot-master/) is the unit’s last and most weighted word, and the preacher should let it fall last.

The pastoral note the unit strikes is therefore neither sentimental nor triumphalist, and the discipline is to keep it from becoming either. It does not pretend the dark is not dark. It does not promise that the dark will be easy, or brief, or explicable. It announces that the dark is not ultimate and cannot win — and it announces this before the conflict has even been shown, because the evangelist disclosed the outcome at the threshold rather than at the end. That is why it is a word a congregation can actually be given, and given in any darkness, without waiting for the darkness to lift first: the light is at home in it, and it has not been overcome.

## The forward arc

The unit hands its work forward on every register the Prologue runs, and naming those hand-offs is the last work of the passage.

**Across the Gospel.** The four steps planted here are grown across the whole book. The life that raises the dead is given to the Son to have in himself (5:26); the light of men is declared the light of the world (8:12; 9:5); the light comes into a world that loved the darkness rather than the light (3:19); and the summons is given to walk while the light is present, lest the darkness overtake (12:35). A reader who has climbed this staircase is equipped to hear the Gospel’s whole light-and-darkness drama as the unfolding of what these two verses first name — which is also to say that a teacher who preaches that drama without these verses has left his hearers to meet it without its ground.

**To the far pole of the foyer.** Structurally the unit stands at the far pole of the Prologue’s opening bracket. Its light-and-life is the terminus of a pairing whose other end is the Word made flesh whose glory is beheld, and [the one life this commentary has traced](/commentary/john/1-4-to-5/14-one-life-arc/) — from the ground of creation to the consummation — reaches its close within this volume only there, at verse 14, where the light of verse 4 is disclosed as glory. That the two verses are a deliberately composed pair, an inclusio the evangelist built rather than a resonance a reader hears, is held here with confidence but is not yet closed; it belongs to the treatment of verse 14, and the commentary carries it forward as a commitment rather than as a conclusion.

**Into the second movement.** Immediately ahead the unit hands into the Prologue’s second movement. The darkness that here neither knew the light nor overcame it will there be shown refusing it in the concrete — the world that “knew him not,” his own who “received him not” — and the question this unit deliberately withheld opens as the drama of that movement: who receives the light, and in what mode. Whether that reception runs first through Israel, and how a fallen will is turned toward the light at all — by grace, rather than by its own choosing — are questions the treatment of verse 11 will take up. They are not answered here, and the restraint is deliberate: the unit says what the light *is* and *whose* it is before it says anything about who received it.

The darkness’s completed failure is, already, the first note of a victory the Gospel will narrate all the way to an empty tomb. Before any witness has spoken and before any rejection has been suffered, the outcome has been given. From here the story of light and dark begins to be told.

> Eternal God, in whose Word was life: before there was any darkness to shine into, the life was in him, and that life was the light of men. You did not send your light to stand off at a distance from our dark, but to shine within it, entering the very condition of those who did not know it. Teach us to say honestly how dark the dark is, and to say in the same breath that it is not ultimate and has not won. Give us the light by which we were made to see; and while we have it, let us walk. For the darkness did not overcome it, and from here the story begins to be told. Amen.
