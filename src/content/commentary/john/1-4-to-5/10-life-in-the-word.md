The exegesis is finished; the theology begins. Four clauses have been worked in the order the evangelist built them, and each was made to answer for itself — [what the first says](/commentary/john/1-4-to-5/06-ontology-life-in-him/), and the second, and the third, and [the last](/commentary/john/1-4-to-5/09-soteriology-not-overcome/). The work of this page is of a different order. It does not return to the clauses to press them further. It asks what the church, on the strength of what they say, has learned to confess — and it takes the treads in the order the composition set them, so that the first tread yields the first thing confessed.

The first tread is the deepest, and the doctrine it grounds is a doctrine of life. What the exegesis fixed at ἐν αὐτῷ ζωὴ ἦν was that the ζωή of verse 4a is the divine, uncreated life — in Barrett's phrase, the essential energy of the Word[^barrett] — and that everything which has come to be has its life *in him*. Two things follow, and they are precise. The first concerns what kind of thing a creature's life is. The second concerns where in the Gospel this claim is going.

## Life held, not owned

Begin with the negative, because it is the one most easily lost. Life is not an attribute the creature possesses in itself. Whatever lives, lives on a life that is not its own in the sense of originating with it or resting on it; the clause locates that life somewhere else — *in him* — and the locative is not decorative. The exegesis showed the preposition shifting deliberately from verse 3 to verse 4: creation came to be *through* the Word, and it lives *in* him. The first preposition names an act; the second names a standing condition. What the doctrine takes from that shift is that creaturely life is *held*, not owned.

And held continuously, not once. This is where the tense earns its keep. An imperfect naming an abiding state, predicated of what has come to be *as it now stands*, will not carry the picture of a deposit made at the beginning and thereafter possessed independently. Calvin saw the point and made it the whole of his comment on the verse: having taught that all things were created by the Word, John "now attributes to him, in the same manner, the preservation of those things which had been created." In the creation of the world there was not, Calvin writes, "merely displayed a sudden exercise of his power, which soon passed away," but something manifested in the steady and regular order of nature; the Word's life-giving power "causes them to remain in their condition," and were it not that "his continued inspiration gives vigour to the world, every thing that lives would immediately decay, or be reduced to nothing."[^calvin] That is the doctrinal cash value of the locative and the imperfect together. To be alive is not to have been given life; it is to be being given it.

## One life, under two relations

The claim needs guarding on two sides at once, and the exegesis supplies both guards, so neither has to be imported.

On one side, the confession must not turn creaturely life into divine life. That the creature's life is held in the Word does not make the creature's life the Word's own life. The relation runs one way and is asymmetric by construction: the divine life is the source, created life the participation. There is one divine life here, under two relations — his own, as the life that is his; the creature's, as the life that is held. Two relations to one life is not two lives, and it is not one life shared out as though it were divisible.

On the other side, the confession must not collapse the life *in* him into the Word's own self-existence. The exegesis was careful here: the imperfect of verse 4a stands at a register distinct from the imperfect of verse 1, and the life predicated at 4a is [the life the Word gives to the world, not simply the life he shares in himself with the Father](/commentary/john/1-4-to-5/06-ontology-life-in-him/). The doctrine keeps that distinction where the grammar put it. What is confessed is not that creation participates in the divine aseity, but that the life creation has, it has in the One whose life is underived.

Both guards are one guard, seen from two sides: the ordering is real, and it does not run backwards.

## Life is already christological

Now the positive weight, and it is considerable. If life is held in the Word, then life is not a neutral substrate. It is easy to read the Gospel as though creation supplied a floor — bare existence, religiously indifferent — on which grace afterward builds something else, so that the Word's work begins where the world's natural life leaves off. Verse 4a forecloses that reading before it can start. To be alive at all is to hold one's life in the Word through whom all things were made. There is no stratum of creaturely existence that stands outside that holding and waits to be reached.

This is why the movement of the unit is not a change of subject when it turns to the light. The same life, turned toward humanity, is [its light](/commentary/john/1-4-to-5/07-anthropology-light-of-men/) — and the staircase makes the identity explicit by taking ζωή up again at the head of the next clause. Had life been a neutral ground, the light would arrive from outside it. Because life is what verse 4a says it is, the light is the shining of what was already there.

## The seed of 5:26

The second thing that follows is a matter of where this claim goes. The Prologue's statement is a seed, and the Gospel grows it in a sentence that says the same thing from the Son's side: "as the Father has life in himself, so he has granted the Son to have life in himself" (5:26). The Prologue says the life *was in* the Word; the Gospel will say the Son *has* it in himself, and gives it. It is one claim at two stages of disclosure — which is why the ζωή of 1:4 can be read as the first word of a field the Gospel develops to its last page, and not as a cosmological preliminary that the soteriology later replaces.

One boundary belongs on that reading. To say that the life is the Son's to give is not yet to say how it is given, or to whom, or on what terms. The reception of the light — who received it and who did not, and what it is to be born of God — is the Gospel's own later work, and this unit deliberately holds it. The locus states the ground, not the grant.

## The register of the claim

The doctrine rests on the ordered reading of ζωή that the exegesis reached, and it is held at the strength the exegesis holds it: a firm and directional preference for the divine-anchored sense, with the creational reach fully affirmed as derived rather than coordinate.[^ordering] The development adds no new exegetical risk to that, because it adds no new claim about the text — it states, at the doctrinal register, what the clause already predicates. Barrett supplies the lexical core; Calvin supplies the continuity, and it is worth noting that Calvin's own interest at the verse runs to the preserving relation rather than to the lexical question, so the two are complementary rather than overlapping witnesses.[^calvin] What would shift the locus is only what would shift the reading of ζωή beneath it, and that reading is settled.

## The pastoral hearing

None of this is remote. The life of every person is, at this moment, a life held in the One who made them — whether or not they know it, and whether or not they would own it if they were told. That is a statement about the reader, not only about creation in general. And it is why the light that this life becomes is not an alien imposition on humanity. When the light shines, it is not a foreign claim arriving from outside a self-contained life; it is the shining of the very ground of one's being, addressing the creature at the point where the creature is already held.

What the first tread teaches the church to confess, then, is this: that life is not ours to have, that it is his to give and to keep giving, and that we are alive on his life at every moment we are alive at all. The clauses say more, and the church confesses more on their strength. That the humanity so held is the humanity constituted to receive this light as light — the second tread's doctrine — is where the unit's theology turns next.
