The resolutions set out across this unit — the working text and its punctuation, the composed architecture of the staircase and the run of tenses, the reading of each of the four clause-treads, and the five theological developments that rest on them — were not reached in isolation. Each was formed in sustained engagement with prior scholarship, read at close range, and each was adjudicated with the strongest alternative readings the literature offers held openly against it. This page gathers that record for John 1:4–5. It names the sources that bore weight on the unit's conclusions, the methodological disciplines under which they were weighed, and — for every principal resolution — the kind of evidence each source supplies, so that the reasoning behind a settled reading can be traced rather than received as assertion.

That commitment governs how the apparatus throughout this Commentary is to be read, and it continues the discipline set out in the scholarly record of John 1:1–3. The source engagement is layered, and at every resolution the layer at which a source is brought to bear is disclosed. Primary-text Tier 1 engagement is full substantive engagement with a source read directly at the point where it bears load. Primary-text register is substantive engagement with a source read directly, but at a supporting register — a critical apparatus, a reference grammar, a standard-reference work — rather than at the load-bearing locus. Adjacent-not-direct engagement is substantive on a neighboring question while remaining at apparatus level on the one presently at issue. Citation-chain engagement reaches a voice through another scholar's citation rather than through the source read directly, and is always named as such. And the published-secondary register marks a source engaged through its published treatment where an independent primary-text treatment of the same evidence takes precedence. One further discipline runs beneath all five: the record keeps its silences. Where a source says nothing at a locus, nothing is routed to him there, and the silences that matter — the places where a natural witness might have been expected to speak and does not — are recorded as part of the evidence rather than papered over.

## The Load-Bearing Sources

The sources that bore weight on this unit fall into four registers: the modern commentary tradition, the compositional and colometric scholarship, the ancient readers and the textual witness, and the Reformed-tradition systematic theology.

Across the whole of verses 4–5, the load-bearing modern source is Rudolf Schnackenburg, engaged directly at every tread: on the sense of ζωή and the sense of φῶς, on the strophe analyses his Prologue chapter surveys, and — decisively for the final clause — on κατέλαβεν, which he reads as "grasp," not "overcome," equating the darkness's failure at verse 5 with the world's not-knowing at verse 10 and his own's not-receiving at verse 11.[^schnackenburg] D. A. Carson supplies three things this unit leans on: the foyer image under which the whole Prologue is read, with the grid that maps each of this unit's treads to its later development in the Gospel; the "rhythmical prose" reading of the linked clauses; and, at verse 5, the verdict that the clause is "a masterpiece of planned ambiguity" — a formulation the soteriology tread takes up directly.[^carson] Herman Ridderbos — in his commentary and in his 1966 *Novum Testamentum* article on the Prologue's structure — is the strongest single witness that the unit's run of tenses is composed: the six past tenses of verses 1–4 read from the perspective of one who looks at the present in light of its origin, the present "shines" that "can hardly relate to a closed period in the past," and, at the final clause, the kerygmatic rendering "the darkness has not *understood* it," which this Commentary's reading of κατέλαβεν stands with.[^ridderbos] John McHugh's critical commentary supplies the uncreated-ζωή reading and the light *par excellence* — φῶς κατ' ἐξοχήν — of verse 4, the resultative force of the perfect at the verse 3/4 seam, and, at verse 5, two precisions the cosmology tread is built on: that the evangelist wrote ἐν and not ἐπί, the light shining *in* and not merely *upon* the darkness, and that the light is "perpetually shining" while the darkness remains.[^mchugh] Raymond Brown supplies the staircase-parallelism characterization of the Prologue's compositional engine, the treatment of ζωή in both his notes and his appendix on the word, and a strophe arrangement of the Prologue that this Commentary surveys without adopting.[^brown] C. K. Barrett supplies the reading of verses 1–5 as a single movement, the text-critical verb decision at verse 4, the characterization of the life as "the essential energy" of the Word, and — at κατέλαβεν — the observation that John is playing on the two meanings: "The darkness neither understood nor quenched the light."[^barrett] Rudolf Bultmann is engaged at two distinct points: his account of the Prologue's chain-locking of sentences — the compositional mechanism this Commentary receives at the final-form level while declining the hymn-source explanation it accompanied — and his verdict on the darkness, that it is "neither a substance nor the sheer power of fate," but "the revolt against the light."[^bultmann] J. Ramsey Michaels marks the shift from imperfect to present at verse 5 as the writer returning to his own time; Leon Morris supplies the observation that the continuous imperfect is used of the Word "again and again" across the Prologue while the present tense is used of him only once — the "shines" of verse 5.[^michaels-morris] Andrew Lincoln supplies the positive placement that guards the cosmology tread: John writes *within* a developed Jewish light-and-darkness contrast that is already "a modified dualism in which God, on the side of light, is ultimately in control and will triumph in the end" — a perspective the Fourth Gospel, he says, shares.[^lincoln] Craig Keener's introduction to the light-and-darkness language is engaged at the adjacent register, mapping the breadth of the ancient usage the unit's vocabulary enters.[^keener] B. F. Westcott supplies the present-continuous reading of φαίνει and the coexistence-paradox — "Light and darkness coexist in the world side by side" — together with the fullest nineteenth-century apparatus for the punctuation question.[^westcott] And Karl Barth reaches the comprehend-reading of κατέλαβεν by a road none of the exegetes takes — a dogmatic-exegetical one: the darkness "had no power to appropriate it, to make it its own, to cease to be darkness and itself to become light," a rendering he holds as his own while expressly ruling the "overtake" sense of John 12:35 "not possible here."[^barth]

On the unit's compositional architecture the witnesses are deliberately held in two separate columns. The figure — the four-tread staircase — is anchored by the colometric and philological scholarship: Dan Nässelqvist's study of the ancient colometry, which grounds both the punctuation this Commentary adopts and the *gradatio* the unit climbs, and Ed. L. Miller's identification of the staircase construction across verses 1, 4, and 5.[^nasselqvist-miller] The tenses — the run from imperfect through present to aorist — are anchored by Ridderbos and by Herman Waetjen, whose aspectual analysis of the run supplies the present as continuous actuality and the aorist as constative, "a line reduced to a point."[^waetjen] Daniel Boyarin's study of the Prologue supplies the chain of -λαβ- verbs — κατέλαβεν, παρέλαβον, ἔλαβον — that binds verse 5 to verses 11–12 and supports the comprehend-register of the final clause.[^boyarin] The Genesis register — the shared vocabulary of light, darkness, and shining that sets this unit inside the Prologue's engagement with Genesis 1 — is carried by Carlos Sosa Siliezar, with Maarten Menken on the transposition of the Genesis concepts from the physical to the spiritual level and Elaine Pagels on the Prologue as exegesis of Genesis 1; Craig Koester supplies the threefold register of the Johannine darkness the cosmology tread holds together.[^genesis] C. H. Dodd bears load at four distinct points: the light-and-glory pole-content that joins verse 4 to verse 14; the verdict that "the cosmological dualism of i. 5 is not ultimate"; the double reading of the whole passage from verse 4 as at once a description of the created order and of the ministry of Jesus; and the non-identity of John's reading of light and darkness with Gnosticism.[^dodd]

The ancient readers are engaged at two levels, and at the first of them this Commentary carries primary-text work of its own. The editor's 1974 collation of Gregory of Nyssa's text of John — original work on the Nyssen citations, supervised by Gordon Fee — supplies the substantive-reception evidence that the Prologue was received as a connected sequence, John 1:1 with 1:4–5, 1:9, 1:14, and 1:18 read as one Logos-life-light movement; and at verse 5 it records Gregory's singular reading, the aorist ἔλαμψε — "shone" — against the present φαίνει of every New Testament manuscript, a reception-note the text-critical page weighs without any change to the working text.[^roades] Origen supplies the earliest sustained clause-reception: the deliberate life-then-light ordering of the two predications, and — bearing directly on the cosmology tread's modified dualism — the insistence that "men are not such by nature," the darkness no part of the creature's created constitution.[^origen] Cyril of Alexandria reads κατέλαβεν cognitively — the originate nature failing to *know* its Light — and stands at the head of the reception this Commentary's comprehend-foregrounded reading joins; the wider reception of verse 5 — Ambrose, Gregory of Nazianzus, Augustine, Isaac of Nineveh — is engaged through Elowsky's collection, where the two-sense fork in the verse's ancient reading is visible on the page.[^cyril-accs] The text-critical decisions rest on H. A. G. Houghton's 2025 companion to the sixth edition of the United Bible Societies' text — the {A}-rated imperfect ἦν at verse 4 against the variant present, and the ancient punctuation question at the verse 3/4 boundary — with Westcott's apparatus and Nässelqvist's colometric argument bearing on the same crux, and the editor's Nyssen collation supplying the reception-side note.[^houghton] At the grammatical register, Edwin Abbott's *Johannine Grammar* supplies the aorist-perfect distinction at the seam, the adversative force of the second καί of verse 5 — "and *yet*" — and the step-construction with its triple tense-shift; Zerwick–Grosvenor flag the verse 4 variant and give the darkness of verse 5 its "ethical content"; Murray Harris's exegetical guide calibrates the punctuation options and carries the adversative καί; Daniel Wallace's syntax supplies the grammatical categories — the plenary genitive and the generic article — under which this Commentary reads τῶν ἀνθρώπων, an application that is the Commentary's own; and A. T. Robertson's *Word Pictures* supplies the linear present — "the light keeps on giving light" — and the moral register of the Johannine darkness.[^grammars]

The theological development is articulated in the Reformed-tradition register. John Calvin's commentary on John was read directly across the unit and is foundational to the theology of the five treads; Herman Bavinck's *Reformed Dogmatics* supplies the systematic doctrine of the whole person as the image of God — the anthropological frame within which the light-of-men tread's doctrine is developed.[^calvin-bavinck]

## The Methodological Discipline

Four disciplines governed how these sources were weighed.

The first is the supersession principle, carried forward from the John 1:1–3 record and operative in this unit at the Gregory of Nyssa loci. Where the editor's original collation of the Nyssen text of John is primary-text work, it takes precedence over the published secondary treatment of the same witness — James Brooks's monograph in the New Testament in the Greek Fathers series — at the load-bearing loci: here, the connected-unit reception of the Prologue and the singular ἔλαμψε at verse 5, where Brooks's reconstruction omits the connected units and rejects the singular reading. The precedence rests on the originality of the primary-text collation, and it is bounded exactly as before: outside the loci where the collation is original work, the published treatment retains its normal authority.[^supersession]

The second is the final-form discipline: receive the observation, decline the explanation. The literature this unit engages includes a long tradition of compositional reconstruction — Schnackenburg's survey of the strophe proposals, Brown's own strophe arrangement, Bultmann's hymn-*Vorlage* with its chain-locking mechanism, and the wider hymn-tradition scholarship surveyed with them. What those reconstructions *observe* — that the material is elevated, rhythmic, patterned, and groupable — this Commentary receives in full, at the level of the received text. What they *explain* — that the pattern is the residue of a pre-Gospel source — it declines, on grounds argued at the [coordinate articulation](/commentary/john/1-4-to-5/04-coordinate-articulation/): the schemes differ substantially among themselves, and the pattern is fully intelligible as the evangelist's own composition.[^finalform]

The third is the co-primary discrimination, and it is this unit's distinctive methodological contribution to the series. The unit's architecture is read as two coordinate dimensions — the synchronic figure and the diachronic run of tenses — held co-primary, and the discipline is that neither borrows the other's witnesses. The figure is anchored by Nässelqvist and Miller; the tenses by Ridderbos and Waetjen; and the split is enforced in both directions — Waetjen, who anchors the aspect of the run, himself disclaims all staircase analysis, and he is cited for the tenses and never for the figure. A convergence manufactured by letting one column's witnesses count for the other would be no convergence at all.[^coprimary]

The fourth is the discipline of recorded silences. No structural or compositional claim in this unit is routed to a source the record holds silent on composition — the systematic and dogmatic-exegetical voices engaged substantively elsewhere in the unit are not enlisted for the architecture, and where an expected witness does not treat a locus at all, the absence is recorded rather than filled. The apparatus this Commentary rests on distinguishes, source by source, what each was engaged *for*; a source engaged for one thing is not quietly credited with another.[^silences]

## The Engagement-Layer Disclosure

With the layers named and the disciplines set out, the record can be read at the level of the unit's individual resolutions.

On the working text — the imperfect ἦν at verse 4a and the sentence-boundary at the verse 3/4 seam — the adopted readings rest on the critical-edition apparatus (Houghton) at the primary-text register, on the colometric argument (Nässelqvist) at the load-bearing level, and on Westcott's apparatus; the reception-side note — Gregory's singular ἔλαμψε — rests on the editor's collation at the primary-text level, with the published secondary treatment engaged as the position superseded. The full decisions are set out in the [text-critical notes](/commentary/john/1-4-to-5/01-the-text/).

On the staircase — the four-tread *gradatio* built by anadiplosis — the reading rests on Nässelqvist and Miller at the load-bearing level, with Brown's staircase-parallelism characterization and Bultmann's chain-locking received at the final-form level, and the strophe reconstructions of Schnackenburg, Brown, and the hymn-tradition scholarship surveyed and declined as explanation.

On the run of tenses — imperfect, present, aorist as one composed movement — the reading rests on Ridderbos and Waetjen at the load-bearing level, with Michaels and Morris supplying the tense-observations that converge on it and Abbott the step-construction with its triple shift.

On the coordinate articulation — shape and spine as two dimensions of one composition — the reading is the Commentary's own synthesis, resting on the two witness-columns above under the co-primary discipline, with no witness counted twice.

On the ontology tread — the life that was in the Word — the reading rests on Schnackenburg's treatment of the ζωή-question, McHugh's uncreated life, Barrett's essential energy, and Brown's word-study, with the ordered two-relation reading — the divine life as source, participated life as gift — the Commentary's own resolution of the question the witnesses jointly pose.

On the anthropology tread — the life as the light of men — the reading rests on McHugh's φῶς κατ' ἐξοχήν, on the grammatical categories supplied by Wallace under which the genitive is read, and on Origen's early witness to the deliberate life-then-light ordering; the imago-Dei development belongs to the [theology of the second tread](/commentary/john/1-4-to-5/11-image-and-light-of-men/), where Bavinck bears its systematic weight.

On the cosmology tread — the light shining in the darkness — the reading rests on McHugh's ἐν-not-ἐπί and perpetual shining, Westcott's coexistence-paradox, Koester's threefold darkness, and the three guards that fence the modified, non-ultimate dualism: Bultmann by exclusion (a revolt, not a substance), Dodd by exclusion (the dualism "not ultimate"), and Lincoln by positive placement (a modified dualism with God ultimately in control), with Origen's "men are not such by nature" carrying the ancient form of the same boundary and the Genesis register (Sosa Siliezar, Menken, Pagels) framing the whole.

On the soteriology tread — the darkness's double failure at κατέλαβεν — the comprehend-foregrounded, both-senses reading rests on Schnackenburg's grasp-equation and Ridderbos's kerygmatic "understood" at the load-bearing level, with Boyarin's -λαβ- chain binding the clause forward, Cyril's cognitive reading at the head of the ancient reception, Barth's dogmatic-exegetical road to the same rendering, Barrett's two-meanings observation and Carson's planned ambiguity naming the deliberateness, and the overcome-register carried by John's own usage at 12:35 with Dodd, Lincoln, Robertson, Westcott, Morris, and the Greek patristic tradition reading it there.

And on the five theological developments, the articulation rests on Calvin, read directly and foundational throughout, and on Bavinck at the systematic register of the image of God, with each tread's exegetical witnesses carrying forward into the doctrine developed on it.

The reader who wishes to follow any of these resolutions further will find, on the page where each is argued, the full apparatus at the point of decision. The works engaged are gathered below.
