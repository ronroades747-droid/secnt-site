The exegesis is behind us, and so are four doctrines. The first tread said that [the life is held and not owned](/commentary/john/1-4-to-5/10-life-in-the-word/) — that no creature has life in itself, and that to be alive at all is to hold one's life in the Word. The second said that [the light is humanity's own](/commentary/john/1-4-to-5/11-image-and-light-of-men/), because humanity is the creature made in God's image and so fashioned to receive this light *as* light. The third said that [the light shines within a darkness that is real, still dark, and not ultimate](/commentary/john/1-4-to-5/12-light-within-darkness/) — the Word at home in the very condition of those who do not know him. And the fourth said that [the darkness could neither comprehend the light nor put it out](/commentary/john/1-4-to-5/13-darkness-cannot-master/), so that the security of the light's mission stands disclosed before the mission is narrated.

This page adds no fifth doctrine. Its work is to say what the four are *together* — and that is not a summary. The evangelist did not set four separate claims side by side and leave the reader to add them up. He built a staircase, each tread taking up the last word of the tread before it, and a staircase is one structure. What it carries, read as one structure, is a single life: one life, traced from its eternal ground to its final consummation. That tracing is where the theology of this unit completes itself.

## The staircase is one structure

The unit's shape is the argument here, so it is worth recalling exactly what the shape is. [The four treads are joined by anadiplosis](/commentary/john/1-4-to-5/02-gradatio-and-foyer/) — ζωή taken up as ἡ ζωή, τὸ φῶς taken up as τὸ φῶς, τῇ σκοτίᾳ taken up as ἡ σκοτία — so that no tread begins a new subject. Each begins by repeating the term the previous tread ended on. The figure is not decoration; it is the evangelist's way of saying that these four things are one thing, examined at four depths.

Read that way, the unit says something a list of four doctrines cannot say. The life that is *in* the Word (verse 4a) is the same life that, turned toward the human race, *is* their light (verse 4b); that same light is what shines *within* the world's darkness (verse 5a); and it is that same shining which the darkness neither grasped nor overcame (verse 5b). Four prepositions and one subject. Not a life, and then a light, and then a conflict — but one divine life, followed through four relations: in the Word, for the world, within the dark, unovercome.

And [the tense-architecture runs the same course diachronically](/commentary/john/1-4-to-5/03-macro-tense-run/) that the figure runs synchronically. The two imperfects of verse 4 hold the eternal ground; the present of verse 5a holds the shining, open and continuing; the aorist of verse 5b gathers the whole of the darkness's opposition into one completed fact. Shape and spine trace the same movement — which is why this unit's theology is more than the sum of its loci. The composition itself is making a claim, and the claim is that there is one life here, not four topics.

## The arc, from its ground to its end

Follow that one life to each of its ends, and the reach of the unit becomes visible.

At the near end stands the *protological* pole. Verse 4a does not say that the Word possessed a life among other properties; it says that in him was life, and the third verse has just said that everything which came to be came to be through him. The life named here is therefore [the ground of created existence as such](/commentary/john/1-4-to-5/06-ontology-life-in-him/) — not a gift added to creatures already standing, but the condition on which anything stands at all. Whatever else this arc will be said to reach, it begins at the beginning: before the world, and as the reason there is a world.

At the far end stands the *eschatological* pole, and the Gospel itself supplies its name. The life this unit calls ζωή, John will call ζωὴ αἰώνιος — eternal life — and the distinctive thing about eternal life in this Gospel is that it is not only promised but *given now*. Dodd puts the Johannine emphasis precisely: the evangelist agrees that the believer will enter into eternal life at the general resurrection, but holds that truth to be of less importance than the fact that the believer already enjoys it, the future being a consequence of the present rather than its replacement.[^dodd-life] So the far pole is not simply distant. The life that grounded creation is the life given to the one who believes, and its consummation at the restoration of all things is the completion of a possession already real. The arc runs from creation to consummation, and it is one life at every point of the run.

That is the whole of it, and it can be said in a line: the life that made the world is the life that saves it, and they are not two lives.

## The ground of everything the Gospel will say about light

Once the arc is seen whole, a good deal of the Gospel that follows reads differently.

Everything John will say about light and darkness is the unfolding of what these two verses first name. The light that comes into the world and is loved less than the dark (3:19); the light of the world that men are called to follow (8:12; 9:5); the summons to walk while the light is present (12:35); the life that the Son has in himself and gives to whom he will (5:26) — none of these is a fresh theme introduced when the narrative needs it. Each is the one life of verse 4, met further down the road. The Prologue does not preview the Gospel's light-and-darkness drama; it *grounds* it, and the ground is protological. What begins in the Gospel's fourth verse is not a motif but a life, and the drama of belief and unbelief that follows is the drama of what that life meets when it shines.

This is also why the unit reads as it does at the level of address. A reader who has climbed the staircase has been told, before a single character has appeared, that the life in question is his own life, held; that the light in question is his own light, proper to him as a creature made for it; that the darkness in question is his own condition, entered rather than avoided; and that the outcome is not pending. Whatever the Gospel narrates after this, it narrates to a reader already placed inside the arc.

## Where the arc does not yet close

One honest qualification belongs here, and this commentary would rather state it than let it pass.

Within the Prologue itself the arc does not reach its end. Its end is consummation, and the Prologue does not narrate a consummation. What the Prologue *does* give is a nearer terminus, and it stands at verse 14: the Word became flesh, and — in the words the evangelist chose — "we beheld his glory." [That verse is the far pole of the bracket in which this unit sits](/commentary/john/1-4-to-5/02-gradatio-and-foyer/), and what the two poles share is not a resemblance of language but an identity of substance. The light of verse 4 is disclosed at verse 14 as glory: one divine self-disclosure, no longer visible radiance but glory apprehensible to the faculty of faith.[^dodd-glory] The life that was in the Word, and that became the light of men, is beheld in flesh — and the beholding is the nearest thing to a terminus the Prologue's first movement can reach from where it stands.

Two things must be held apart here, and the commentary holds them apart deliberately. That the light of verse 4 and the glory of verse 14 are one self-disclosure is *established*: it is grounded directly in the sources and this commentary carries it as secure. That verses 4 and 14 were *composed* as a deliberate frame — an inclusio the evangelist set to bracket the Prologue — is a claim of a different order, held here with high but provisional confidence, because it is not yet attested by any source this commentary has engaged. Its confirmation is reserved for the commentary's own treatment of verse 14, where the frame can be closed from the other end.[^frame] The reader should therefore take the arc's within-volume terminus as strongly read and openly unfinished. The arc itself — creation to consummation — does not depend on it; what depends on it is the claim that the evangelist built the two verses to answer each other.

## The register of the claim

Like the second tread's doctrine of the image, this gathering is theological illumination drawn from the unit's architecture; it is not load-bearing on any of the clause-level resolutions, and none of them was framed to establish it. [The four treads carry their own arguments, with their own witnesses, at their own places](/commentary/john/1-4-to-5/09-soteriology-not-overcome/), and nothing here reopens any of them.[^settled]

What this page adds is a reading of the whole: that the unit's four doctrines are four relations of one life, and that the life so traced runs from the ground of creation to the consummation of all things. Our research holds that reading with high confidence, for the plainest of reasons — the gathering is not an inference laid over the text but the composition's own claim. The evangelist joined the treads by catchword, ran one tense-sequence through them, and set the whole at the mouth of a Gospel that will spend twenty-one chapters unfolding it. To read the four as one is to read them as they were built. The single element held at lower confidence is named above, and it is the compositional frame at verse 14, not the arc.

## One life

So the unit's theology ends where its exegesis began, with a single subject examined from four sides.

In him was life — that is the ground, and nothing that lives stands outside it. That life was the light of men — that is the turn toward us, and the dignity of the creature made to receive it. The light shines in the darkness — that is where the light is actually found, at home in a condition it did not create and does not pretend away. And the darkness did not overcome it — that is the outcome, disclosed before the conflict, and the end of the matter.

One life: in the Word, for the world, shining in the dark, unovercome. The commentary has now said what this unit teaches. What remains is to set out the scholarly record on which the reading rests, and to ask what the unit asks of the one who hears it.
