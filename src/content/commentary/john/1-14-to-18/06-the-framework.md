The previous section closed the Prologue's frame and ended on a question: whether the Prologue's parts are folded around a centre. This section answers it. The shape of the whole was left open when the frame was first settled, [where the Prologue begins](/commentary/john/1-1-to-3/02-foyer-bracket/), and it is decided here. The Prologue is three movements in progression, the third returning to the first. It is not a concentric structure built around a centre. What it carries are real concentric figures, and they are carried by a line. The brackets settled at the Prologue's opening stand under this framework as they would have stood under either answer.

## Two readings of the shape

There are two ways to read the shape of John 1:1–18. On the first, the Prologue moves forward: from the Word in the beginning, to the Word coming as light, to the Word's glory in the flesh, and the last movement comes back to the first. On the second, the Prologue is a chiasm. Its members match from both ends inward, the first with the last, the second with the second-to-last, and so on, until they meet at a centre on which the whole is folded.

The chiastic reading has a long history. McHugh, after Culpepper, traces it to N. W. Lund in 1931. Lund set verses 6–8 and 15 aside as "extraneous material" and paired verses 1–2 with 18, 3–5 and 9–10b with 16–17, and 10c–12 with 14, around verse 13 at the centre. McHugh judges the attempt "too elaborate to be convincing".[^lund] M.-É. Boismard's form of it pairs verses 1–2 with 18, 3 with 17, 4–5 with 16, the two passages about John the Baptist with each other, and 9–11 with 14, leaving verses 12–13 at the centre.[^boismard] The best-known form is R. Alan Culpepper's, which works toward the middle through seven matching pairs and makes verse 12b "the 'pivot' on which the chiasm turns, the centre of attention", in Carson's report of it.[^culpepper] Other forms centre the fold on verse 14. Lund's and Boismard's proposals have been read here in their own publications. The others are engaged through the commentators who report them and answer them.

## The chiasm of the whole declined

A chiasm of the whole is declined, and this commentary holds the decline with very high confidence. Four of the commentators it reads decline such a chiasm in their own voice, each with his own hedge. Two more decline Lund's proposal. And none adopts a chiasm of the whole.

Raymond Brown says it plainly: "We remain in doubt on the applicability of a chiasm pattern to the Prologue."[^brown] Köstenberger grants that the second passage about the Baptist "seems to speak against a linear progression", doubts whether the New Testament has chiasms on this scale, and then says of the Prologue: "The argument that 1:12 forms the heart of such a chiasm is tenuous as well."[^eec] Daniel Boyarin: "I remain less than persuaded by most suggestions of an all-inclusive chiastic structure for the Prologue."[^boyarin] Craig Keener grants that Boismard's inclusio, the Word as God in verses 1 and 18, is "very likely", but judges that "his chiasmus is forced", and the structure he offers of his own he offers "without assurance that it is correct".[^keener]

Bultmann and McHugh decline Lund's proposal, the first of them, and neither goes further. McHugh is the more accommodating of the two. He calls Boismard's construction "impressive" and allows that one of the proposed plans may help a reader follow the argument.[^mchugh]

Carson's verdict is the most favourable, and it is recorded with its own hedge. Of the many proposals, he finds Culpepper's "one of the most believable (though still not entirely free of difficulty)".[^culpepper] He does not adopt it. His own conclusion turns from the chiasm to the line, as the next section shows.

## A line carrying figures

The positive framework is held with high confidence. Ridderbos supplies its criterion: "But the criterion for the prologue's structure is not its literary form but the Johannine pattern of thought, which is one of return and repetition of the same motifs and of progressive expansion of the field of thought."[^ridderbos] The Prologue goes forward, and as it goes it takes up again what it has already said.

Carson's sentence, whole: "Whether or not John intended his readers to find a chiasm in his Prologue, he clearly expected them to detect a certain progression in his line of thought."[^carson] Köstenberger: "Thus, the storyline seems to progress in linear fashion from 1:1 to 1:14 to 1:18".[^eec-line]

The framework is held below very high confidence for one reason. Its second half, the concentric figures, rests on a narrower base than the decline does: on Ridderbos's pattern of return, and on the frame itself as the previous section set it out.

## What the chiastic tradition saw

What the chiastic tradition saw is received, and what it built on that is not. Brown grants that Boismard's arrangement "is supported by some valid observations (the concluding vs. 18 picks up the theme of the opening vs. 1; and vs. 15 matches vss. 6–8)".[^brown] Those observations are the outer pairs, and both are received. The first is the frame. The chiastic tradition draws it as verses 1–2 with verse 18. It answers to the bracket the commentary settled at the Prologue's opening, verse 1 with verse 18: *the Word was with God*, and *the only Son, himself God, who is in the bosom of the Father*. The second is the two passages about John the Baptist, verses 6–8 and verse 15, set at matching points in the second movement and the third. These are real concentric figures, one inside the other.

The concentric design of the whole is declined, and with it the inner pairings: verse 3 with verse 17, and verses 4–5 with verse 16. Brown finds those two parallels "highly imaginative".[^brown] The decline of the pairing of verse 3 with verse 17 is not a decline of what [the previous section](/commentary/john/1-14-to-18/05-frame-completed/) received. There, verse 17 was heard to echo verse 3 in a single verb, ἐγένετο, *came to be*, inside the frame. Here what is declined is a composed pair, matched point for point as a member of a chiasm. The two are different figures, and the one does not carry the other.

So no centre is left for the Prologue to fold around, at verse 14, at verse 12b, or anywhere else. The commentary has met the pivot at 12b before, in [its treatment of the second movement](/commentary/john/1-6-to-13/06-gospel-facing-register/). Its answer there was that the reading is right about the weight and wrong about the shape: the weight was there and the shape was not. That answer is now a framework for the whole: what was said of one verse is said of the Prologue.

## How firmly this is held

- **The decline of a chiasm of the whole**: very high confidence. Four commentators decline it in their own voice, two more decline Lund's proposal, and none adopts it.
- **The framework, a linear movement carrying concentric figures**: high confidence, below the decline because the concentric figures rest on a narrower base.
- **The outer pairs, the frame and the two passages about the Baptist**: received from the chiastic tradition.
- **The inner pairings and the centre**: declined, at verse 14, at verse 12b and everywhere else.

The figures are real, and the whole is not folded around a centre. One more pair remains to be weighed: the light of verse 4 and the glory of verse 14. That is the next section's question.
