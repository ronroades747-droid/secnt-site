The four pages before this one each took up one claim the confession of verses 14 to 18 makes: that [the Word became flesh](/commentary/john/1-14-to-18/17-the-incarnation/), that [his glory was beheld](/commentary/john/1-14-to-18/18-dwelling-and-glory/), that from his fullness the church has received [grace in place of grace](/commentary/john/1-14-to-18/19-grace-law-and-truth/), and that [the only Son has made God known](/commentary/john/1-14-to-18/20-god-made-known/). This page adds no fifth claim. It asks what the four are together, and this commentary's answer is one life. [Verses 4 and 5](/commentary/john/1-4-to-5/14-one-life-arc/) began the account of that life, and [verses 6 to 13](/commentary/john/1-6-to-13/21-advent-in-retrospect/) carried it on. Each page marked where the account was going and left it open there. Here the arc the whole Prologue has traced is gathered on one page.

What follows gathers what the pages before it held, at the strength they held it. Nothing in it reopens the reading of any verse.

## The one life

The account follows the light's revelatory reach. *In him was life, and the life was the light of men* ([verse 4](/commentary/john/1-4-to-5/11-image-and-light-of-men/)). *The light shines in the darkness* ([verse 5](/commentary/john/1-4-to-5/12-light-within-darkness/)). He was *the true light … coming into the world* ([verse 9](/commentary/john/1-6-to-13/11-true-light-coming/)). And at verse 14, *we beheld his glory*. It is one life at every point, the life that was in the Word, and the glory beheld is that same light, now seen in the flesh. That the glory of verse 14 is the light of verse 4 is held with high confidence, as a claim about what the verses say. That the evangelist composed the two verses to answer each other is held with high but provisional confidence, as this commentary's reading. The page on verses 4 and 5 left that question for verse 14, and [it has been weighed there](/commentary/john/1-14-to-18/07-foyers-inner-pair/): the pairing was assessed, and it was not raised.

**Where it ends within the Prologue.** Within the Prologue the account ends where the light is seen: ἐθεασάμεθα τὴν δόξαν αὐτοῦ, *we beheld his glory*. Its condition is ὁ λόγος σὰρξ ἐγένετο, *the Word became flesh*. The Word arrives in the flesh, and the beholding follows from it. Dodd reads the sentence in that order: the Logos, the place of both life and light, became flesh, and the beholding of his glory was what followed, which he says the evangelist adds.[^dodd] That the account ends at the beholding, with the enfleshment as its condition, is this commentary's own reading. It is the editor's ruling, as [the reading of verse 14](/commentary/john/1-14-to-18/11-glory-beheld/) records, and it was made on a close weighing.

## Two ends, kept apart

The Prologue's end is not the Gospel's. As [the commentary on verses 6 to 13](/commentary/john/1-6-to-13/11-true-light-coming/) read it, the light was always present to the world it made, and its coming is one advent toward perception. That is held with high confidence and no higher. The perception reaches its fullness beyond the Prologue, at the hour of the Son's glorification: the Gospel's end point, and not the Prologue's. In the Gospel Jesus names it himself: "The hour has come for the Son of Man to be glorified" (12:23).[^hour] At verse 14 that end point is deepened, and not moved. Verse 14 settles what the perception is: the glory perceived in the flesh, through the signs, by those given to see, during the ministry. That the full perception comes at the hour is held with high confidence, as [the reading of verse 14](/commentary/john/1-14-to-18/11-glory-beheld/) holds it.

So two ends are in view here, and this commentary keeps them apart. Within the Prologue the account ends at the beholding. Its full perception lies in the Gospel, at the hour. The figure on this page draws the first solid, inside the Prologue, and the second dashed and dimmed beyond its edge. The two are not one.

The Prologue's end has a tense-form, which [the gathering of the passage's verbs](/commentary/john/1-14-to-18/16-the-tenses-gathered/) set out: a single becoming that conditions a prolonged beholding, each told as one completed whole.[^tense] Its reach goes further than its first witnesses, carried by the present of verse 15 and the *we have all received* of verse 16. That reach is not verse 14's own tense-form, because this commentary reads the "we" of verse 14 as the eyewitnesses. The tense-form is held with high confidence.

## Across the movement

The account's end is seated across [the movement's three parts](/commentary/john/1-14-to-18/03-the-articulation/): the confession of verse 14, the witness of verse 15, and the ground and the declaration of verses 16 to 18, with verse 18 closing the third part rather than standing as a fourth. The seating is this commentary's own reading of what the exegesis settled, and each part of it is held as firmly as that part was held.
- **The confession** of verse 14 is the end itself: *the Word became flesh*, and *we beheld his glory*.
- **The witness** of verse 15 carries it into the reader's day. *John bears witness about him*, in the present, and [the witness still sounds](/commentary/john/1-14-to-18/04-temporal-payload/). It is [the movement's one opening](/commentary/john/1-14-to-18/12-witness-who-still-speaks/) onto the reader's present. That the witness sounds in the reader's day is held with very high confidence, and that it is the movement's one such opening, with high confidence.
- **The ground and the declaration** of verses 16 to 18. The ground is the receiving the church has had: *from his fullness we have all received — grace in place of grace*, and *the grace and the truth came to be through Jesus Christ*. Both are held with high confidence. The declaration is the end's completion, in the Son's making known: *he has made him known*. The telling is complete in that it is finished; complete is not exhausted, as [the page before this one](/commentary/john/1-14-to-18/20-god-made-known/) set out. That verse 18 crowns the movement is held with high confidence.

[The frame that pairs verses 1 and 18](/commentary/john/1-14-to-18/05-frame-completed/) closes in verse 18, and that is held with high confidence. With it the movement comes back to where the Prologue began. The Word who was with God in the beginning is the Word in whom the life was, and the account that set out from that life returns to him at the movement's close. That the account so returns is this commentary's reading of what the settled readings show together.

## What the confession hands on

The confession tells the incarnate life as a completed life, and it does not leave the reader outside it. The Word who was became flesh and was beheld. The eyewitnesses' beholding is past, and the earthly life they saw is complete. The witness that speaks of it and the receiving from his fullness are not past. The witness to him still sounds, and it reaches the reader's day. The receiving goes on, because what is received is grace in place of grace, from his fullness, and [the supply does not fail](/commentary/john/1-14-to-18/19-grace-law-and-truth/): the church receives still. And the Son who has made known the God no one has ever seen is, as he always is, in the bosom of the Father.[^abiding] How firmly each is held:
- **The one life**, from the life in the Word to the glory beheld, ending within the Prologue at the beholding, with the enfleshment as its condition: this commentary's own reading, on the editor's ruling. **The glory of verse 14 as the light of verse 4:** high, as to what the verses say. **The two verses composed to answer each other:** high, provisionally.
- **The light always present, its coming one advent:** high, and no higher. **The full perception at the hour**, the Gospel's end point and not the Prologue's: high. **The end's tense-form:** high.
- **The seating across the movement:** this commentary's reading of what the exegesis settled. **The witness in the reader's day:** very high. **The witness as the movement's one opening:** high. **The receiving, grace in place of grace:** high. **The grace and the truth come in Christ:** high. **Verse 18 as the movement's crown:** high. **The frame closed in verse 18:** high.
- **The Son in the bosom of the Father, as he always is:** high.

[^dodd]: C. H. Dodd, *The Interpretation of the Fourth Gospel* (Cambridge: Cambridge University Press, 1953), p. 206: "The climax of the series of statements about the eternal light in the Prologue is the statement that the Logos, the τόπος of both life and light, σὰρξ ἐγένετο, and the effect of this, the evangelist adds, was that ἐθεασάμεθα τὴν δόξαν αὐτοῦ (i. 14)." And p. 207, "in consequence of the incarnation of the Logos, i.e. of the eternal Light, ἐθεασάμεθα τὴν δόξαν αὐτοῦ, δόξαν ὡς μονογενοῦς παρὰ πατρός." Dodd speaks of a series and its climax, not of an account of one life or of its end. That the account ends at the beholding is this commentary's reading, not his.

[^hour]: John 12:23 is quoted from the Berean Standard Bible.

[^tense]: Murray J. Harris, *John* (Exegetical Guide to the Greek New Testament; Nashville: B&H Academic, 2015), p. 35: ἐσκήνωσεν "may be ingressive (“took up residence”) but is more probably constative (“lived/dwelt [among us, ἐν ἡμῖν]”) like ἐγένετο and ἐθεασάμεθα", "with ἐγένετο denoting a single occurrence (the incarnation = conception of Jesus Christ) and ἐσκήνωσεν and ἐθεασάμεθα referring to prolonged action." Of the six ἐγένετο in the Prologue he writes that "these uses of the aorist are all constative, denoting action, whether momentary, prolonged, or repeated, that is conceived of as a unit" (p. 22). That the becoming conditions the beholding is this commentary's reading, not Harris's.

[^abiding]: Murray J. Harris, *Jesus as God: The New Testament Use of Theos in Reference to Jesus* (Grand Rapids: Baker, 1992), p. 99, in his reply to readings that find in εἰς an earlier entrance into the Father's bosom, or the Son's return through the ascension to his preincarnate state: "the preceding ὁ ὤν probably depicts a supratemporal condition that had no beginning". Setting out that reading of the participle, he adds: "In itself ὁ ὤν need say nothing about the relationship of the Son to the Father during Christ’s earthly ministry" (p. 96). This page does not read the incarnate life off the participle.
