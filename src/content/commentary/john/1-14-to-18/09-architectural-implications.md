The architecture of this movement has now been read whole: its edges, its parts, its tenses, its frame, its framework, its inner pair, and its one opening. Seven sections took it apart a layer at a time. This one asks what the assembled shape teaches, before a single verse is expounded.

The question is a real one. A passage's architecture can be described exactly and still be treated as scaffolding, useful to the analyst and set aside once the verses are reached. What follows is the claim that this movement's shape is not scaffolding. Told from its completion, the movement is the life of the Word who became flesh: the confession that he dwelt among us and was beheld, the witness that the one who comes after the Baptist was before him, the fullness received, the grace and the truth that came to be through Jesus Christ, and the Son who alone has made the unseen God known. The parts are the evangelist's composition. Even the witness, whose words are the Baptist's, stands where the evangelist set it. And the close is his: the Baptist's words end with verse 15, and verses 16–18 are the evangelist's own.

## What a shape can teach

Four things follow from the composition before any verse is read closely. They are set out here as what the shape already carries, not as conclusions the exegesis will reach. The verse-by-verse work that follows will reach its own, and where it does, the ground will be the words of each verse. What is claimed here is narrower and prior: that a reader holding only the outline of this movement, and none of its detail, would already be holding four things.

The order matters. Each layer of the architecture was settled on its own evidence, in its own section, without reference to what the layers might jointly teach. What follows is read off that result, not built toward it.

## The confession is attested, then grounded

The first is that the confession of verse 14 is not left to stand alone. *And the Word became flesh and dwelt among us, and we beheld his glory, glory as of the only one from the Father, full of grace and truth.* That is the movement's first part, and it is spoken in the first person plural, by those who beheld. The evangelist sets a witness beside it at once, *John bears witness about him, and has cried out*, and gives the confession its ground only after the witness. Two clauses opening with *for* carry the ground: *For from his fullness we have all received — grace in place of grace*, and *For the law was given through Moses; the grace and the truth came to be through Jesus Christ.* As [the commentary on the articulation](/commentary/john/1-14-to-18/03-the-articulation/) set out, verse 17 grounds verse 16 and verse 16 grounds the confession, and the catchwords run across the witness: what verse 14 says the Son is full of, verses 16 and 17 say was received and came.

What the shape teaches here is the confession's footing. The "we" who beheld his glory speak again at verse 16, and there the plural widens: *we have all received*. The company of those who saw him is widened to the whole believing church, which has received a share from the fullness of grace and truth that verse 14 named in the Son. The receiving is wider than the beholding. And what the church has received did not arise from the church. It came to be through Jesus Christ, an event in history, set against the law that was given through Moses. The confession stands attested by a witness and grounded in what has been received from him, and the movement's shape is what puts it there.

## The movement closes on God made known

The second concerns where the movement comes to rest. Its crown is the declaration of verse 18: *No one has ever seen God. The only Son, himself God, who is in the bosom of the Father — he has made him known.* The verse stands without a connecting particle. It declares what the two *for* clauses ground, and it closes the movement inside its third part.

Each of the movement's great moments has its place in that order. The enfleshment of verse 14 is the condition of all that follows, for there is nothing to behold until the Word has become flesh. The receiving of verses 16 and 17 is the ground the church has had. The declaration of verse 18 is the completion, the Son's making God known. That the Word became flesh remains the climax toward which the Prologue has been moving, and nothing here takes that from it. The crown is another thing: the place where this movement closes, and it closes on the knowledge of God.

And it closes the Prologue on God named. The Prologue began with the Word who was God, *and the Word was God*. It ends by naming the only Son God, *the only Son, himself God*. As [the commentary on the frame](/commentary/john/1-14-to-18/05-frame-completed/) set out, the θεός of verse 1 and the μονογενὴς θεός of verse 18 enclose the Prologue, and what the opening said of the Word the close has shown.

## The reader is addressed through a witness

The third concerns how the movement reaches its reader. It tells a completed life, from its completion, and only once does the telling open onto the reader's own present. That once is the Baptist's voice: *John bears witness about him, and has cried out*. The present and the perfect set his witness in the evangelist's own day, and [the section on the aperture](/commentary/john/1-14-to-18/08-the-aperture/) found it to be the movement's one opening onto the reader's present. The reader of this movement is reached through a witness who still testifies.

The second movement of the Prologue reached its reader at another place. There, as [the commentary on verses 6–13](/commentary/john/1-6-to-13/09-architectural-implications/) found, the one opening onto the reader's present was at the believing, *to those who believe in his name*, a door left open at the end of the turn. Here the opening is a witness's voice. Each of the two movements opens onto its reader at one place, and the places are different. Whether the Prologue's movements, taken together, make a pattern of such openings is a question for the volume's introduction, and it is not answered here.

The movement's other break in time is at verse 18, and it is of another kind. *No one has ever seen God* holds without exception and for all time, and the participle says where the Son always is, in the bosom of the Father. That break does not open the telling onto the reader's present. It states what holds outside the movement's narrative time. The two breaks differ in kind, and this section keeps them distinct.

## The Prologue has gone out and come back

The fourth concerns the shape of the whole Prologue, as this movement completes it. The Prologue is not a pattern folded on itself. As [the commentary on the framework](/commentary/john/1-14-to-18/06-the-framework/) set out, it is three movements in progression, the third returning to the first, and it is not built around a centre. A chiasm of the whole, every part answered by its mirror around a midpoint, is declined. The Prologue still carries concentric figures: its last verse answers its first, and the two passages about the Baptist answer each other. But the figures sit inside a movement that goes forward.

So the Prologue has gone out and come back. It went out from the Word who was with God in the beginning. It has come back to the only Son, who is in the bosom of the Father. The return is the movement's and not the Son's, for the Son has not been away from where he is: the participle of verse 18 says where he always is.

## How firmly this is held

Nothing in this section adds a claim of its own. Each of the four carries the confidence of the layers it rests on, and those were set out in the sections before this one.

- **The confession attested and then grounded**: the seams of the articulation, very high confidence; the church that receives as the company of those who saw him, widened, high confidence.
- **The movement closing on God made known**: the crown at verse 18, high confidence; the θεός inclusio, high confidence, as a figure of the text; the reading of verse 18 on which it rests, high confidence, held provisionally.
- **The reader addressed through a witness**: the witness in the reader's day, very high confidence; the witness as the movement's one opening onto the reader's present, high confidence; the second movement's one opening at the believing, very high confidence; whether the openings make a pattern of the whole Prologue, a question for the volume's introduction.
- **The Prologue gone out and come back**: the decline of a chiasm of the whole, very high confidence; the frame's closing in this movement, and the framework of a linear movement carrying concentric figures, high confidence.

What the shape teaches is therefore held firmly where it rests on how the movement is composed, and no more firmly than the readings of verse 18 where it rests on them.

These four are taken up again in this commentary's theology, after the verses have been expounded, where the movement's theology is drawn out in its own loci. Here they are named as what the shape already teaches, so that the verses are heard inside it. The exegesis begins at the next section, with the Word who became flesh.
