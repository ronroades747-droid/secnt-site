The four pages before this one each took up a single doctrine: [the world that did not know its Maker](/commentary/john/1-6-to-13/17-world-and-its-maker/), [his own who did not welcome him](/commentary/john/1-6-to-13/18-israel-first-not-only/), [the right to become children of God](/commentary/john/1-6-to-13/19-right-that-carries-effect/), and [the begetting that is God's own act](/commentary/john/1-6-to-13/20-word-gives-god-begets/). This page adds no fifth doctrine. Its work is to say what the four are together. They are, finally, one movement, seen from where the evangelist stands.

## Where the reader stands

The evangelist tells this movement from the far side of an advent that has already come, and the reader stands there with him. From that place the light's whole career is read backward: the witness who came, the light that was always coming, the world that did not know, the home that did not welcome, the ones who received and were begotten. [The temporal plan of the movement](/commentary/john/1-6-to-13/04-temporal-payload/) is built that way, from a completed advent looking back.

And then the reader finds the one verb in the movement whose time reaches the reader's present: τοῖς πιστεύουσιν, *to those who believe*, at the end of verse 12. [The section on that phrase](/commentary/john/1-6-to-13/07-the-aperture/) shows where it is set and why the other present forms in these verses do not do the same work. The movement closes its narration and leaves its door open.

Two readers, from very different places, give the register of that open door, and their words stand as they are. Bultmann, on the present tense at verse 5, speaks of "the revelation which has been given to the world through this work and now lives on in the community."[^bultmann-present] And Calvin, summing up his comment on verse 10, writes that "never was Christ in such a manner absent from the world, but that men, aroused by his rays, ought to have raised their eyes towards him."[^calvin] Neither is speaking of the participle at verse 12. Carrying their words to the door is this page's step.

## Three things, for the reader now

Seen from where the evangelist stands, the movement locates three things for the reader.

The light's coming is *finished*. The advent is told from its far side, and nothing in the reader's present waits for it. The reader is not among those the light was still approaching, but among those to whom it has come.

The world's refusal is *told*. It belongs to the narrated past, and to the Gospel the Prologue opens. The reader is not asked to re-enact it, but to hear it.

And [the begetting](/commentary/john/1-6-to-13/15-begotten-of-god/) is *done* wherever the believing is. It is [one completed act, coincident with the reception](/commentary/john/1-6-to-13/16-the-units-tenses/), and the tense encodes no ordering of the believing and the being begotten. God's act is the ground, and the priority is a priority of ground, not of time. So the reader's present is not a waiting for a birth, but a believing in which the birth is already the ground.

## The one life, continued

This is the second movement's part of an arc the first movement opened. [At verses 4 and 5](/commentary/john/1-4-to-5/14-one-life-arc/) the Prologue traced one divine life: the life in the Word, become the light of humanity, shining into the dark, and not overcome. That page followed the life from its ground in creation to its consummation, and called the life one life at every point. Here the arc runs on. [The true light coming into the world](/commentary/john/1-6-to-13/11-true-light-coming/) at verse 9 is its next point; the refusal at verses 10 and 11, the reception at verse 12, and the begetting at verse 13 follow.

What this movement supplies is the arc's *already*. The status of God's children is, in Ridderbos's words, "a totally new mode of existence … which as 'eternal life' has already been initiated by the work of Christ."[^ridderbos] The life the first movement called ζωή, John will call ζωὴ αἰώνιος, eternal life, and it is here already begun. Even Bultmann, who of all these readers is least disposed to trace this arc, takes the term in an "eschatological" sense: man becomes God's child "only when he has been transferred into a new existence." Of the two forms he finds that idea taking, he derives John's from the one that "occurs in the present", "and not from Jewish eschatology."[^bultmann-eschatological]

The begetting is how John names the way that life is begun. And the present participle at the end of verse 12 holds the arc open.

## Where the arc does not yet close

The arc's nearer end lies beyond this movement. It is verse 14, where the light of verse 4 is disclosed as glory, and the Word becomes flesh. Dodd allows that the evangelist knows a reception of the Logos apart from the incarnation, and then says of it that "this is a given possibility for mankind only because the Logos is He who became flesh."[^dodd] That verse belongs to the Prologue's next movement, and it is engaged there. Nothing here asserts it. This page names the reception's part of the arc, and marks where it is going.

## The register of the claim

Like [the gathering at the close of the first movement](/commentary/john/1-4-to-5/14-one-life-arc/), this page is theological illumination, not a load-bearing claim on any verse. None of the readings of the verses was framed to establish it, and nothing here reopens any of them.

What it gathers is held where its own pages hold it. That the movement's one opening onto the reader's present stands at the believing is held with very high confidence. So is the grammar of the begetting, one completed act coincident with the reception. That the light was always coming, and that the reception reaches beyond the enfleshed arrival, rest on [the working calibration adopted at verse 9](/commentary/john/1-6-to-13/11-true-light-coming/), and are held with high confidence and no higher. The gathering itself — that the four are one movement — is the shape the movement was composed to set before the reader.

## The pastoral hearing

An advent finished, a refusal told, a begetting done. And one door, at the believing, left open by design.

The reader's present is not a waiting. It is a believing, and the birth is already its ground.
