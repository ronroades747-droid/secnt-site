The exegesis has read the verse, and the theology turns to what it teaches. [The verse of the begetting](/commentary/john/1-6-to-13/15-begotten-of-god/) was read clause by clause: who were begotten — not of bloods, nor of the will of the flesh, nor of the will of a husband — but of God. This page does not argue those clauses again. It asks what the church, on the strength of them, has learned to confess. The close of the movement yields a doctrine of regeneration, and the doctrine is carried to the exact edge the text draws, and no further.

Three questions follow. What the church confesses regeneration to be. How the Word's giving and God's begetting are distributed. And how God's act meets the believing that receives him.

## What the begetting is

What the begetting *is*, the verse says. It is God's own creative act, with no cooperating cause. It is not the union of two parents, not the impulse toward it, not a husband's decision, and not anything of the class those three stand for — every way a human being is brought into the world, made to stand for everything the world can do.

It effects a real change of kind, and it issues in a new mode of existence.[^ridderbos-mode] And it is bounded at both ends. Below, by the claim [verse 12](/commentary/john/1-6-to-13/14-the-turn-children-of-god/) made about τέκνα: a real filiation, not a reckoning. Above, by participation — in the gift, and not in the being. Ridderbos gives the upper bound its form: the new life of the children of God is "a participation, not in the divine being, but in God's imperishable gift."[^ridderbos-participation] Cyril of Alexandria held both bounds in one passage, with the christological reason attached: the grace conferred on believers gathers, as it were, "into a kinship of nature that which was alien from God the Father", and yet they are not of the Father's own essence, as the Only Begotten is.[^cyril-bounds] Children in fact, and never as the Son is.

## What it is not

What the begetting is *not*, the verse also says.

It is not baptism. The Fathers read verses 12 and 13 in baptismal settings. But that is the verse's reception, and not its referent. A text preached at a baptism is not thereby [a text about baptism](/commentary/john/1-6-to-13/15-begotten-of-god/).

And it is not the virginal conception. The resemblance is real — real enough to have generated a variant reading, in the singular, of Christ. It is answered on its merits, and [the plural text](/commentary/john/1-6-to-13/01-the-text/) stands.

## The Word gives, and God begets

The distribution of agency is the text's own. The Word gives the right; God begets; and the giving issues from the begetting. Barth states the order: "As the proclamation of a right it issues from existent divine power and its exercise. The Word summons them to be what they already are from God".[^barth-summons]

The God who begets is the God of [the Prologue's opening verses](/commentary/john/1-1-to-3/07-pros-ton-theon/), and not the Word. But the Prologue does not yet call him the believers' Father. That title waits for 20:17, and it is not anticipated here.

## Father, Son and Spirit

Verse 13 names the source of the begetting and withholds its mode. The Gospel supplies the mode itself, at 3:5–8: begotten from above, of water and Spirit, the Spirit blowing where it wills.

Read with verses 12 and 13, the three texts distribute one act across the persons. The Father is the source — ἐκ θεοῦ, of God. The Son is the giver of the right — ἔδωκεν, he gave. The Spirit is the mode of the begetting, as the Gospel tells it at 3:5–8.

This commentary sets that out as illumination, and settles nothing on it. Verse 13 names the source and withholds the mode, and to import the Spirit into verse 13 is to say more than the verse says.

## How God's act meets faith

What the doctrine does hold is the distinction between God's constituting of his children and the human turning to him, as the text carries it.

God's act comes first, as the *ground*. The priority is marked three ways. The voice shifts, from the active verbs of verse 12, where the people receive and believe, to the passive of verse 13, where God is named as source. There is the completed aorist. And the giving issues from the begetting. But the manner in which that act meets the believing is not told.

Carson observes that "these verses refrain from spelling out the connection between faith and new birth".[^carson-refrain] This commentary keeps the same restraint. A doctrine of regeneration built on John must be built with it. It asserts the priority of grace as ground, and it refuses to schematize a mechanism the evangelist declined to give.

## The register of the claim

The doctrine rests on what [the exegesis](/commentary/john/1-6-to-13/15-begotten-of-god/) held, and is held where the exegesis held it. The grammar of the begetting is held with very high confidence. That it is a creative act, issuing in a new mode of existence, is held with high confidence and no higher, because it rests on verse 12's finding that the status is not confined to the enfleshed arrival — itself held with high confidence under [the working calibration adopted at verse 9](/commentary/john/1-6-to-13/11-true-light-coming/). The two agents are held with high confidence at each of their parts. The distinction between God's act and the human turning is held with high confidence, and the priority of God's act with high confidence and no higher. That the verse is not about baptism, and not an allusion to the birth of Jesus, is held with high confidence.

The distribution across Father, Son and Spirit is not a further finding. It is set out, and left as it is set out.

## The pastoral hearing

The pastoral hearing is plain. To become a child of God is not something anyone can produce, inherit or decide — and it has been done, for as many as received him.

How that one act stands within the whole advent the passage tells, and the door it leaves open, is where the theology turns next.
