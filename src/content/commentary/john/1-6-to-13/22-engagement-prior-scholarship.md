The readings set out across this unit were not reached in isolation. That holds for the working text and its one crux, for the bounded movement and the three beats inside it, for the verses read one by one, from the parenthesis to the begetting, for the run of tenses that tells the advent in retrospect, and for the five theological developments that rest on them. Each was formed in sustained engagement with prior scholarship, read at close range, and each was settled with the strongest alternative the literature offers held openly against it. This page gathers that record for John 1:6–13. It looks back over the finished readings: which sources bore weight, the disciplines under which they were weighed, and, resolution by resolution, the kind of evidence each source supplied, so that the reasoning behind a settled reading can be traced rather than received as assertion. The [unit's opening page](/commentary/john/1-6-to-13/) set out the same sources in advance of the readings; this page is their record once the readings are made.

The record continues the discipline of the scholarly records of [John 1:1–3](/commentary/john/1-1-to-3/19-engagement-prior-scholarship/) and [John 1:4–5](/commentary/john/1-4-to-5/15-engagement-prior-scholarship/), and it discloses, at every resolution, the layer at which each source was brought to bear. *Primary-text Tier 1* engagement is full substantive engagement with a source read directly at the point where it bears load. *Primary-text register* is direct engagement at a supporting register — a critical apparatus, a reference grammar, a standard reference work. *Primary-text-synthetic* engagement reads a summary articulation that carries the author's authority but not the underlying analysis; the layer stands in the project's discipline, and nothing in this unit was engaged at it. *Citation-chain* engagement reaches a voice through another scholar's citation rather than through the source read directly, and is always named as such. *Adjacent-not-direct* engagement is substantive on a neighbouring question while remaining at apparatus level on the one at issue. Two further marks complete the record. The *published-secondary* register marks a source engaged through its published treatment where an independent primary-text treatment of the same evidence takes precedence. And the record keeps its silences: where a source says nothing at a locus, nothing is routed to it there, and the silences that matter are recorded as evidence rather than papered over.

## The Load-Bearing Sources

The sources that bore weight on this unit fall into four registers: the modern commentary tradition, the ancient readers and the textual witness, the grammatical apparatus, and the Reformed-tradition systematic theology.

Across the whole movement, the load-bearing modern source is Rudolf Schnackenburg, engaged at every verse and, at the Baptist verses, against his own interest. His source theory would have let him treat those verses as an intrusion, and his verdict on the text as it stands is short: "The evangelist put them here deliberately." His objection from the aspect of the participle governs the reading of verse 9; his reading of the unit's three sections on the Prologue's present structure, stated inside his source-critical chapter, became the movement's second architectural carrier; and his account of faith as grace, read whole in his excursus on the notion of faith, stands behind the objection at ἐξουσία that this commentary answered rather than outvoted.[^schnackenburg] John McHugh's critical commentary supplies the unit's fullest dossiers: the asyndeton that marks the turn, the τὰ ἴδια / οἱ ἴδιοι dossier, the witness-office as testimony in a great trial, and, at the crux, the versional evidence and the case for the singular, laid out and answered.[^mchugh] Karl Barth's record is the clearest case of disagreement disclosed. He was read at the highest register in eight of the unit's nine settled readings; the ninth, the reading of the unit's architecture, stands apart under the composition fence this commentary keeps on him, lifted for that reading alone. Over those eight, the record shows him engaged against where he narrows πάντα at verse 9 and hears σάρξ as hostile at verse 13, and adopted where he defines the witness by his own negation, renders ἐξουσία as *right*, and reads the Word's giving as concentration rather than exclusion.[^barth] Rudolf Bultmann, engaged from the scans, is this unit's most instructive witness against interest: his survey of every Johannine ἐξουσία carries the lexical reading at verse 12, he reads verses 6–8 and 15 as the text the narrative of 1:19–51 comments on, and his reading of the present at verse 5 — the revelation that "now lives on in the community" — is carried by this commentary to the open door at verse 12, while his hymn source is surveyed and declined.[^bultmann] Herman Ridderbos carries the three-movement architecture entire: the second cycle taking up the categories of the first, verse 11 as the place where "the prologue has reached its low point", the reception as a new mode of existence already begun, and verse 13 set to guard the refusals of verses 5, 10 and 11.[^ridderbos]

Around that core the commentary tradition was engaged wherever a locus required it. D. A. Carson supplies the pace of the Prologue's linking words and their thinning from verse 12b, and the restraint at verse 13 that this commentary adopted.[^carson] Murray Harris's exegetical guide is the grammatical anchor throughout, from the grounds of verse 9's construal to the present participle "open to those who live after John's time".[^harris] Raymond Brown grounds the documentary case at the crux and reads verses 11 and 12 as summaries of the Gospel's two parts; C. K. Barrett reads the singular as an allusion and not as text; and Andreas Köstenberger's *John 1–21* in the *Evangelical Exegetical Commentary*, which enters the series with this unit, supplies the alternation of cadence across the parenthesis, the marked δέ at the turn, and, from verse 14's side and hedged as he hedges it, the sense that it is almost as if all of verses 6–13 forms "one large parenthesis".[^brown-barrett-eec] A second ring — Dodd, Michaels, Morris, Keener, Lincoln and Westcott — was engaged at the highest register where a locus called for it: Dodd on ἐξουσία as what a competent authority gives, Michaels on the present at verse 9, Morris on the parenthesis, Westcott on the crescendo of verses 9–11, Lincoln and Keener on the temporal reference.[^second-ring]

The ancient readers are engaged at two levels, and at the first of them this commentary carries primary-text work of its own. The editor's 1974 collation of Gregory of Nyssa's text of John supplies the reconstruction of verses 5–13 in Gregory, the adaptation-chain running from verse 5 to verse 9 read as christological reception, and the evidence that at verse 13 Gregory is a witness by adaptation only.[^roades] Origen, through Heine's translation, is engaged for the parenthesis and stands as a load-bearing negative at verse 9, where his commentary is lost and the verse survives only as a title.[^origen] Cyril of Alexandria, Chrysostom and Augustine enter at their loci through Elowsky's collection, which carries the patristic reception of the whole unit; the witnesses to the singular at verse 13 are reached through the commentators who report them.[^accs] The text-critical decisions rest on Houghton's companion to the sixth edition of the United Bible Societies' text, engaged for the entry it does not carry.[^houghton]

At the grammatical register, Zerwick–Grosvenor, Robertson, Abbott, Wallace and Waetjen supply the parsings the exegesis composed and, where it had to, placed: ἐξουσία from ἔξεστιν, the effective aorist at verse 12, the light "continually coming", the pendent nominative, and the constative reading of the unit's aorists.[^grammar] And the theological development is articulated in the Reformed-tradition register. John Calvin's commentary was read directly at every verse, and he stands at the highest register twice: at verse 12, where his rendering of ἐξουσία as a *right* — ἀξίωσις, a claim — is attested independently by Barth four centuries later, and at the unit-wide temporal reference, where at verses 6, 10 and 11 he carries the reading of the light's coming in phases in the Reformed line.[^calvin] Herman Bavinck's *Reformed Dogmatics* is recorded but not engaged: none of its four volumes argues any verse of the unit, and the readings it states without argument were weighed against this unit's resolutions and moved none of them.[^bavinck]

## The Methodological Discipline

The sources were weighed under the supersession principle and seven disciplines.

The **supersession principle** governs the Gregory of Nyssa loci. Where the editor's original collation of the Nyssen text is primary-text work, it takes precedence over the published secondary treatment of the same witness, James Brooks's volume in the New Testament in the Greek Fathers series. At verse 13 the precedence runs in both directions: Brooks's reconstruction is superseded where it reaches past what Gregory's adaptations attest and where it stops short of what they do. At verse 9 his apparatus carries no unit for the verse at all, and the reception reading rests on the collation alone. Outside the loci where the collation is original work, the published treatment keeps its normal authority.[^supersession]

**Final-form composition** reads the unit as the evangelist's deliberate composition. The hymn, interpolation and displacement readings of Bultmann, Schnackenburg, Brown and Lincoln are surveyed as accounts of the text's history and not adopted as accounts of its sense, and the break at verse 6 is received as composed contrast. With it goes the move this commentary calls **receive the observation, decline the explanation**, made at three places in this unit: the source-critics' seams at 8 | 9 and 12b | 12c are received as seams while the insertion that would explain them is declined; Bultmann's observation at verse 9 is received while his hymn layer is declined; and the observation behind the reading of the light's coming in phases is received while its frame is declined.[^finalform]

The **co-articulation of the synchronic and the diachronic** reads the movement's rhetorical articulation and its run of tenses as two dimensions of one composition, tracing the same treads on distinct witnesses. The division tradition carries the one and the aspect anchors the other, and neither dimension borrows a datum from the other's register; where a scholar stands in both families, as Barrett and Schnackenburg do, he stands there on different data. Two rules settled in this unit did their work at named sources. The **predicate rule**, settled at verse 10, is what let the commentary keep one referent for ὁ κόσμος with McHugh and Barth and decline Harris's three senses: the aspect belongs to the clause's predicate, not to the noun. The rule that **the word does not carry what the passage carries**, settled at verse 13, is what answers Barth's hostile σάρξ and the decisiveness some hear in the reception aorists: in each case the sense a source assigns the word is supplied by the wider Gospel, and the word is not made to bear it. The **dual posture** receives a source at one register and declines it at another, in the same breath and with the register named: Carson's reading that the evangelist is already setting the stage for the rejection at verse 9 is received as narrative effect and declined as the sense of the word.[^coarticulation]

The last discipline is **the record of silences**. Each source's engagement is drawn from a standing record of what it treats; no claim is routed to a source recorded as silent at the locus; and the silences are kept as evidence. In this unit the record held its silences and three times falsified them on the read: Lincoln, Keener and Sosa Siliezar, none expected to speak to the temporal reference, were found to speak to it, and are recorded as positives.[^silences]

## The Engagement-Layer Disclosure

With the layers named and the disciplines set out, the record can be read at the level of the unit's individual resolutions.

On **the working text**, the unit is read on the critical editions' common text without emendation, verses 6 through 12 carrying no variation unit in any engaged source. The reading rests on the critical apparatus and Houghton at the primary-text register, and on the editor's collation, in which Gregory's citations of verses 9, 11 and 12 carry no reading that diverges from the critical text. At **the crux of verse 13** the plural is read — believers, begotten of God — at very high confidence. The case rests at the load-bearing level on Brown's statement that not a single Greek manuscript supports the singular, on McHugh's laying out of the versional evidence and of the case for the singular, on Carson's argument from the direction of change, on Schnackenburg's observation that an early testimony to the virgin birth would hardly have been removed once present, on Barth's reading of the patristic singulars as free allusions, and on Barrett's argument from the grammar of verse 12. The singular is held against it as a serious minority whose case is chiefly internal. Its ancient witnesses — Tertullian, Irenaeus and the *Epistula Apostolorum*, with Justin only perhaps and Hippolytus questionably — are reached through those commentators, at the citation-chain layer, and Galot's case for it through McHugh. The full decisions are set out in the [text-critical notes](/commentary/john/1-6-to-13/01-the-text/).[^crux]

On **the bounded movement**, the edges at verse 6 and after verse 13 rest on the division tradition from independent methods and from opposite sides of the hymn question — Ridderbos from the final form, Schnackenburg from the source-critical side, Barth hearing at verses 12–13 a first climax, Westcott, Michaels, the *Evangelical Exegetical Commentary*, Keener and Bultmann — and on the structural necessity that the unit's readings were each made with reference to their neighbours. On **the articulation and its three figures**, the seams at 8 | 9 and at the turn rest on Barrett, Lincoln, McHugh and Ridderbos, with the source-critics' insertions received as seams; the one refusal told three times rests on Barth and Bultmann, with Dyer, for whom verses 5, 10 and 11 portray the same event — the Word's coming — and the refusals are its sequel; and the triad of verse 13 answering the refusals rests on Ridderbos's reading of verse 13 as guarding them, the member-for-member answering being this commentary's own. Culpepper's chiasm, centred at verse 12, is answered rather than acknowledged: right about the weight, wrong about the shape. The architecture is set out at the [bounded movement](/commentary/john/1-6-to-13/02-bounded-movement/) and the [articulation](/commentary/john/1-6-to-13/03-articulation-three-figures/).[^architecture]

On **the parenthesis** of verses 6–8, the reading of the Baptist verses as an integral, composed member of the argument rests on Ridderbos, Barth, Michaels and the *Evangelical Exegetical Commentary*, and on Schnackenburg and Bultmann against their own interest; the insertion hypotheses are held against it, and the commentators who deny the verses are poetry while granting the division are received on the commentary's own ground. The witness-office, defined by its own negation, rests on McHugh's great trial, on Ridderbos, and on Barth against Bauer's "only".[^parenthesis]

At **verse 9**, the construal of ἐρχόμενον with the light rests on five converging grounds drawn from McHugh, Carson, Barth and Harris, with Bultmann, who construes the participle otherwise, supplying the continuity of the subject against his own construal, and Schnackenburg's objection from aspect controlling how far the participle can be pressed. The attributive reading of the Authorized Version and of Chrysostom and Cyril is held against it, its antiquity weighed as showing that the ambiguity is real. The light's illumination of every man — given to each, and refused by perception and not by provision — rests on Barth, Carson, Ridderbos and the *Evangelical Exegetical Commentary*, with Chrysostom carrying the oldest form of the account of refusal; Barth's restrictive πάντα is engaged against at full force. The reading of the light's coming as presence that is already advent is the commentary's own synthesis, held at high confidence and no higher, with Westcott, Abbott, Barth, Schnackenburg, Ridderbos and the *Evangelical Exegetical Commentary* supplying its parts. The reading is set out at the [true light](/commentary/john/1-6-to-13/11-true-light-coming/).[^verse9]

At **verse 10**, the integration of three clauses into two beats, concession and adversative, rests on Barth, Ridderbos, McHugh and the *Evangelical Exegetical Commentary*, with Bultmann against interest and Harris for the place of the adversative. The one referent of κόσμος, the human world under three aspects, rests on McHugh, Barth, Ridderbos and Bultmann, with Harris's shift of sense across the three clauses held against it. The world's not-knowing, a refusal of the relation the world owed its maker, rests on Ridderbos, McHugh, Schnackenburg and Dodd, with Barth's verdict that the passage sounds "the melody of triumph" carried as the bound against a tragic reading.[^verse10]

At **verse 11**, the concentric reading of τὰ ἴδια as the Word's own world and οἱ ἴδιοι as Israel rests on Harris and Schnackenburg at the neuter, on the Israel tradition at the masculine, and on Cyril of Alexandria for the concentric form. The homeland reading of McHugh, Westcott, Morris, Barrett and Brown, and the universal reading of both members by Barth, Bultmann and Michaels, are held against it and answered. The welcome refused rests on Barth, Ridderbos, Harris and Schnackenburg, with McHugh received in part.[^verse11]

At **the turn and verse 12**, the pair of verbs across the verse boundary and the marked δέ rest on the *Evangelical Exegetical Commentary*, Harris, McHugh, Barrett, Zerwick–Grosvenor and Michaels, with receiving read as believing. ἐξουσία as a right that carries its effect rests on the concurrence of Calvin and Barth, on Bultmann's survey, on Dodd and on Westcott; Schnackenburg's reading of it as a capacity is held against it and answered from the figure rather than outvoted, and Brown's objection to a semi-judicial right is received in part. τέκνα as a word of derived nature rests on Barth, McHugh and Westcott; and the believing in his name, open to the reader through the present participle, rests on Harris, Westcott, Schnackenburg and Barth, with Barth's verdict on what the verb denotes declined only at that verdict. The readings are set out at [the turn](/commentary/john/1-6-to-13/14-the-turn-children-of-god/).[^verse12]

At **verse 13**, the threefold negation and the begetting of God rest on Barth, Schnackenburg, McHugh, Ridderbos and Carson's restraint, with Cyril of Alexandria for both bounds of the begetting. Barth's hostile σάρξ is the one surviving conflict at the highest register, answered rather than outvoted; Barrett's ordering of the birth after the reception, and the baptismal and virginal-conception readings, are held against it. That the begetting is the ground of the reception and not its sequel in time is this commentary's own reading, disclosed as such. It is set out at [begotten of God](/commentary/john/1-6-to-13/15-begotten-of-god/).[^verse13]

On **the unit's tenses** — an advent told in retrospect, framed by aorists, with imperfects beneath the frame and a present at its open door — the reading rests on Barth's frame and Barrett's vantage, on Harris and Carson for the constative class, on Schnackenburg and McHugh for the one completed act, on Westcott for its coincidence with the reception, and on Barrett and Michaels for what the tense does not order. Calvin carries the reach of the light before the flesh in the Reformed line, and Lincoln and Keener the temporal reference; Waetjen's grammar is received and his reference to the time before Jesus is declined. The reading is set out at [the unit's tenses](/commentary/john/1-6-to-13/16-the-units-tenses/).[^tenses] On **the movement's reach beyond itself** — its frame between the Prologue's opening and its summit, the turn of verses 11–12 as the Gospel's two halves in miniature, and the one door opened at the believing — the reading rests on Barth's ladder and Westcott's form for the frame, on Harris, Brown, Carson, Lincoln and Dodd for the Gospel's two halves, and on Harris and Bultmann for the door. The turn as the Gospel's two halves and the door at the believing are the unit's own composition and are held with very high confidence; the frame, and the Gospel's realization of the turn at 13:1, reach beyond the unit and are held provisionally, as the [aperture](/commentary/john/1-6-to-13/07-the-aperture/) sets out.[^reach]

And on **the five theological developments** — the world that did not know its Maker, his own who did not welcome him, the right that carries its effect, the begetting that is God's own act, and the advent told in retrospect with its door left open — the articulation stands in the Reformed-tradition register, with Calvin read directly at every verse, and with each verse's witnesses carrying forward into the doctrine developed on it: McHugh and Barth for the world, Cyril and Bultmann for his own, Dodd, Westcott and Calvin for the right, Cyril and Barth for the begetting, and Bultmann, Calvin, Ridderbos and Dodd for the advent in retrospect. None of the five is load-bearing for a verse's reading; each is the theology the readings yield.[^loci]

The reader who wishes to follow any of these resolutions further will find, on the page where each is argued, the full apparatus at the point of decision. The works engaged are gathered below.
