Six layers of this movement have now been read, one at a time. This section lays two of them over each other and asks what the overlay is worth.

The two are the ones that carry the movement's shape. [The way the passage is jointed](/commentary/john/1-6-to-13/03-articulation-three-figures/) — a parenthesis about the witness, a hinge-movement where the light comes to its own, a turn at the reception, a close — is one of them. [The way it is timed](/commentary/john/1-6-to-13/04-temporal-payload/) — an aorist frame, an imperfect interior, the aorists of the receiving, a climactic act at the end, with one present inside — is the other. They were arrived at separately, by different means, and this commentary set them out in separate sections for that reason. Laid over the same eight verses, they fall on the same treads.

<!-- diagram -->

## Tread for tread

The correspondence is not approximate. The parenthesis and the aorist frame occupy the same verses. The hinge-movement, where the light is in the world and the world does not know him, runs over the imperfect interior. The turn at *but as many as received him* runs over the aorists of the receiving. The close runs over the climactic act at verse 13. Four beats, four treads, in the same order and at the same places.

Two dimensions of one composition, then: not two rival descriptions of the passage, and not one description told twice. A rhetorical reading answers the question *how is this built*; a temporal reading answers *when does it say these things happened*. That they answer at the same joints is a fact about the text, and it is the kind of fact that can be checked.

## Witnesses that do not overlap

What keeps the correspondence from being an artifact of one reader's habits is that it does not rest on one body of evidence.

The joints rest on the division tradition — the commentators who mark where this unit begins and ends and where it breaks inside, working from the flow of the argument, the connectives and the structure of the sentences. Barrett, Lincoln, McHugh, Ridderbos and Westcott all divide the unit, and the source-critical readings arrive at seams of their own, from premises the others do not share.[^division]

The tenses rest on a different body: the grammarians and commentators who parse the verbs. Harris, Zerwick, Waetjen, Robertson and Wallace are the aspect anchors, and Barrett, Michaels, Carson, the Evangelical Exegetical Commentary and Schnackenburg are the commentators consulted for the sense each verb carries in its place.[^aspect]

These two families were not consulted for the same thing, and they are not doing the same work. The first group is not being asked about verbs and the second is not being asked where the unit divides. Neither dimension borrows a datum from the other's register.

Two names stand in both lists, and the point survives them, because they stand there on different data. Barrett divides the unit at one set of pages and reads its tenses at another. Schnackenburg's seams are not the pages his objection about the verbs stands on — and his readings of the verbs are themselves conditioned on his own division, which he says in so many words.[^both]

## Where the joints do not quite meet

This is the second time the pattern has appeared. [At the movement before this one](/commentary/john/1-4-to-5/04-coordinate-articulation/), the ascending figure of verses 4 and 5 and the run of tenses beneath it traced the same four treads, and the correspondence was set out there for the first time. A second instance is worth more than a repetition, because it can show what a single instance cannot — and this one refined the description in two ways.

The first is that the joints are offset by one clause, exactly where the movement hinges. The parenthesis about the witness closes at verse 8; the imperfect interior opens at verse 8. The clause οὐκ ἦν ἐκεῖνος τὸ φῶς, *he was not the light*, does both jobs at once: it is the parenthesis's one *was*-clause, and it is the first verb of the interior. And the clause after it — ἀλλ᾽ ἵνα μαρτυρήσῃ περὶ τοῦ φωτός, *but that he might bear witness concerning the light* — lies between the two joints and measures the distance between them.

That is worth pausing on, because it is what a hinge looks like when two dimensions cross it. In a single sentence the witness is handed off to the light he came to attest. The rhetorical seam and the temporal seam are not in the same place to the letter; they are one clause apart, and the clause between them is the one doing the handing. A correspondence that were merely imposed would not have produced an offset, and an offset of one clause is not a failure of the correspondence but its most precise moment.

## Where each opens onto the present

The second refinement is that [the one opening onto the reader's own time](/commentary/john/1-6-to-13/07-the-aperture/) is not part of the pattern at all.

In the movement before this one, the present tense sat at the third tread and coincided with the rhetorical turn: the light *shines*, and the turn is there. Here it does not. The present participle at the end of verse 12, *to those who believe*, sits at the turn's end rather than at the turn, and it is marked by its tense where the turn itself is marked by δέ, the *but* that opens the sentence. Two different marks, at two nearby but distinct places.

So the two dimensions coincide in their treads and not in where each opens onto the evangelist's present. That is a narrowing of the claim rather than a weakening of it: the pattern is about the joints, and the aperture is its own thing, placed for its own reasons.

## How firmly this is held, and at which register

Two different questions have been in view across these sections, and they are not held with the same grip.

The first is how this unit is composed — that it is one bounded movement, where its seams fall, that the reception carries the payload of the tense-plan, the form that pairs verse 1 with verse 10, the two halves as a composed summary, and the aperture. That is held with very high confidence, and the reason is not that many readers agree, though many do. It is that [the eight settled readings](/commentary/john/1-6-to-13/02-bounded-movement/) this commentary reaches in the verses that follow were not reached independently of one another. The parenthesis closes where the light resumes; the welcome refused by his own is answered across a verse boundary; the one-sentence close of verses 12 and 13 carries the climactic act the tense-plan has been moving toward; and the frame that opens the whole opens on the very person the parenthesis introduced. Remove the reading of these verses as a single movement and those resolutions do not become separate small problems — they become unintelligible. The movement is what they jointly require.[^necessity]

The correspondence set out in this section adds to that. Two descriptions of the same treads, resting on witnesses brought to bear at different registers, converge — and convergence from independent evidence is worth more than agreement within one body of it.

One question inside the unit is held less firmly. How the interior of verses 9 to 11 is to be read — what exactly is happening there, and to whom — is held at high confidence rather than very high, and capped there deliberately, because the readings available for those verses do not separate as cleanly as the seams around them do. That is treated at those verses.

The second question is where this unit stands in the Prologue as a whole — [the frame](/commentary/john/1-6-to-13/05-the-frame/), [the turn's realization in the Gospel](/commentary/john/1-6-to-13/06-gospel-facing-register/), the pairing of verse 1 with verse 10, and the inversion of the first movement's plan. That is held at a different register and with a different grip: high, and provisional; moderate-to-high at the inversion taken alone. Three reasons are principled rather than a hedge. This second movement presupposes a third, and the third has not yet been read here. The frame's inner pair waits on the treatment of verse 14. And the Prologue-wide readings belong to the introduction to the volume, which seats them. The expected movement of that register is upward, as the third movement is read.

The architecture has now been read whole. What remains is what it carries: the next section asks what this shape teaches.
