The aorist frame — Ἐγένετο (v. 6), ἦλθεν (v. 7) — sets the vantage on this side of a completed advent: a retrospect, not a threshold. Beneath it the imperfect interior — οὐκ ἦν (v. 8), Ἦν (v. 9), ἦν (v. 10) — reaches back across the whole history of the light's presence, the reach drawn dashed and dimmed because the advent has no single verse as its threshold. Then the reception aorists — οὐκ ἔγνω, ἦλθεν, οὐ παρέλαβον, ἔλαβον, ἔδωκεν — five summaries at the advent's climax, and the climactic aorist ἐγεννήθησαν, one completed act coincident with the reception. Two present participles are drawn at their two offices: ἐρχόμενον (v. 9) inside the time-embracing ἦν, not reaching the reader; τοῖς πιστεύουσιν (v. 12) as the one arrow that leaves the past frame toward the reader — the door the completed advent leaves open."
