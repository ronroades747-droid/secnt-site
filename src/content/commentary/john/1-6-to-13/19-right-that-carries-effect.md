The exegesis has read the verse, and the theology turns to what it teaches. [The verse of the turn](/commentary/john/1-6-to-13/14-the-turn-children-of-god/) was read clause by clause: to all who received him, to those believing in his name, he gave the right to become children of God. This page does not argue those clauses again. It asks what the church, on the strength of them, has learned to confess — and the turn yields a doctrine of the believer's standing.

Its shape is fixed by what the exegesis settled at three points: the right, the children, and the name.

## A right, granted

What the receivers are given is a *right*. It is not a power infused into them, and it is not a permission held in suspense, waiting to be used. It is a standing granted by a competent authority, whose pronouncement constitutes the standing it declares. Dodd calls it a grant made "by a competent legislative or judicial pronouncement; in this case, by the authoritative 'word' of the Ruler of the universe."[^dodd-pronouncement] Westcott: not "mere ability, but legitimate, rightful authority, derived from a competent source which includes the idea of power."[^westcott-authority] Calvin: the Evangelist does not mean "any intermediate faculty, or one which does not include the full and complete effect".[^calvin-effect]

The right is conferred by declaration, effectual because of who declares it. And it is a right *to become*. Westcott puts the whole sequence in a sentence: "The fruit is not given at once, but the seed."[^westcott-seed]

## Children, and never as the Son is

The standing is a real filiation. John's word for the receivers is τέκνα, children, and the idea of "child" as against "son" "is that of a community of nature (v. 13) as distinguished from that of a dignity of heirship."[^westcott-nature]

So the church's word "adoption" names the derivative mode of that nature. It is not a substitute for it. The nature is derived from God's begetting, and never from the Son's essential generation, and the children are never as the Son is. Barth gives the ground: "The right of the hosoi to be children of God is thus enclosed and grounded in the Son."[^barth-enclosed]

## The name believed in

The believing that defines the children is believing in his name. In John that means accepting the person for all that he reveals himself to be.[^schnackenburg-acceptance] It is an assent, and not a merely intellectual one. It is a committal of the self, and not a psychology of faith. Its whole power comes from its object.[^barth-object]

And the name is the divine name. [The exegesis](/commentary/john/1-6-to-13/14-the-turn-children-of-god/) followed Barth there, and so the Prologue's confession of [the Word's full deity](/commentary/john/1-1-to-3/08-theos-en-ho-logos/) is here confirmed from the believer's side. To believe in his name is to take him for what the Prologue's first verse said he is.

## Two guards

Two guards keep the doctrine honest.

The first concerns its reach. The status is *not confined to the enfleshed arrival*. The reception is as wide as the coming it answers — one advent, with a covenant career through Moses and the prophets and its direction toward verse 14. "There were and are those" who receive him.[^barth-were] Dodd reads the verse of Israel's faithful remnant, rendering it: "Those who received the word, to them it gave the right to become children of God".[^dodd-remnant] This is held with high confidence and no higher. It rests on [the working calibration this commentary adopted at verse 9](/commentary/john/1-6-to-13/11-true-light-coming/), it is open to refinement when verse 14 is treated, and Brown presses on it. For him the Spirit who begets God's children was not given until Jesus was glorified.[^brown-timing]

The second concerns its certainty. Believing in his name does not certify itself. The same phrase names a faith Jesus would not entrust himself to, at 2:23–24. And the present participle is where John puts the limit: the standing is for those who go on receiving.

## The register of the claim

The doctrine rests on what the exegesis held, and is held where the exegesis held it. That ἐξουσία means a right is held with very high confidence as a reading of the word. That the right carries its effect is held with high confidence; the objection that the word names a real capacity conferred, and that a bare entitlement is too weak, is a serious one, and the reading is held knowing its weight.[^capacity] That the children's standing is "a community of nature" is held with very high confidence, and "adoption" as its mode, with high confidence. That believing in his name defines who the receivers are is held with very high confidence; that the believing is committal, with high confidence, and with the boundary of 2:23–24. That the name is the divine name is a confirmation of what the Prologue already holds, not a further finding.

The first guard is held as it was stated above, with high confidence, on the working calibration. The second is not a further finding at all. It is the text's own limit.

## The pastoral hearing

The pastoral hearing is plain. The child of God is not made by feeling, or by effort, but by a grant — and the grant makes real what it names.

What it is to *become* a child of God, and how the Word's gift meets God's begetting, is where the theology turns next.
