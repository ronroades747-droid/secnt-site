*In the beginning was the Word.* We have read the sentence so often that its verb goes past unnoticed — *was*, the plainest past tense in the language, the word we use for anything that used to be. In John's Greek the verb is ἦν, and it is the imperfect tense. Our research concludes that the imperfect here is doing theological work: it is the tense of unbounded existence, stating what already was without marking any point of origin. When John says the Word *was*, he is not telling us when the Word began; he is telling us the Word did not begin. The verse does not give the Word a date, however early. It says that at the beginning — whichever beginning the reader has in mind — the Word was already there. At whatever beginning you care to name, the Word already was. That is why the dullest-looking word in the verse carries its largest claim: John is not dating the Word's origin; he is denying that the Word has one.

## Pedagogical task

This slide's job is to make one word stop the room. The headline names the surprise in English, the gold word shows the Greek behind it, and the gloss gives your hearers the line to carry out. Use it from the pulpit in that order. Read the verse as you always would; then go back, read it again, and stop on *was*. Tell them it sounds like the dullest word in the sentence — and that in John's Greek it is the imperfect, the tense of what simply was, marking no point of origin. Then give them the line: at whatever beginning you care to name, the Word already was. The restraint: do not teach the tense system from the front — "the imperfect: being, without a beginning" is one sentence, said once, and the rest is here for you; do not count the four *was*es of verses 1 and 2, which is its own lesson; do not set *was* against *came to be* in verse 3, which is another; and do not go on to the verb John withholds from the Word, which is a third. This slide has one verb and one claim. One sentence is the whole move: *stop on "was" — John is not dating the Word's beginning, he is denying that the Word has one.*
