Ask a congregation what John's great title for Jesus is and the answer comes back without hesitating: **the Word**. It is the first thing the Gospel says, and it is how the Gospel is introduced from most pulpits. *In the beginning was the Word.* **What almost nobody notices is that John stops using it.** The title stands at 1:1 and again at 1:14 — *and the Word became flesh* — and after that it is gone. Not once in the twenty chapters that follow. **The Gospel that opens by calling him the Word never calls him the Word again.** That is not carelessness and it is not an accident of style. **Karl Barth described the title as a placeholder** — a *locum tenens*, "the provisional designation of a place which something or someone else will later fill." It can be read before it is understood, the way an inscription can be read before it is grasped, or the way the unknown in an equation can be written down before its value is known. **The Prologue sets the equation; the Gospel solves it.** And Barth noticed precisely where the solving shows: at 1:14, he wrote, "the name of the Logos is not repeated — it finally drops out of the picture — but this human name: Jesus." **Once the Person can be named and narrated, the abstraction has done its work.** Our research concludes that the disappearance is not a loose end in the Gospel but the title finishing its job, and that the hearer's surprise at it is the whole of what this slide is for.

*For the teacher, the shape of it in three steps.* First, **do not explain it — send them looking.** Hand out John's Gospel and ask the class to find every place John calls Jesus the Word, and let them work forward through chapter after chapter without finding it. Second, **let the question come back from them**: *why does he stop?* It is worth more arriving that way than it could ever be worth as your opening line. Third, **give them Barth's image and stop.** Name the Latin once, let it land, and do not gild it.

*What this page does not do, said plainly.* It does not exegete 1:14 — what *became flesh* means, the glory, the tabernacling — and names that verse only as the place where the title is used for the last time. It does not take up the verb that governs the Prologue's opening clauses, or the councils, or the creed. It does not walk the backgrounds the title came from — the Wisdom tradition, the Aramaic paraphrases, Philo — or argue which of them John was reaching past. **It hands over one observation about where a word is and is not, and stops there.**

## Pedagogical task

This slide's job is to **withhold**. It says that the title stops and it shows you the title, and it does not say why — and the withholding is the pedagogy, because a hearer who has been handed a question is in a different posture from one who has been handed an answer. The restraint is that you must not resolve it early: the moment you explain the placeholder before the class has felt the absence, the slide has spent itself and you are simply telling them something. Project it and let it sit. Point at the Greek and say it aloud once — the two words are the whole subject, and they are on the screen so that your hearers have seen the thing whose disappearance you are about to describe. From the front the whole move is one sentence: *the Gospel that opens by calling him the Word never calls him the Word again, and that is not a loose end.* **What not to do beyond that:** do not turn it into a lesson on the Logos backgrounds — the philosophy, the Wisdom literature, the Targums — because that is a different lesson and it will bury this one. And do not let anyone leave thinking John lost interest in the title; the point is the opposite, that the title succeeded. **Two downloads, two jobs.** The **wide slide carries the whole move** — the claim, the Greek, and the reason it stops — and it is the one to project while you teach from it. The **tall slide carries the claim and the Greek without the reason**, which makes it the one for a bulletin, a newsletter column or an invitation: it says the surprising thing and sends the reader to find out why.
