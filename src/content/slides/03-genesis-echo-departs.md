That John begins his Gospel with the first words of the Bible is one of the better-known facts about the Prologue: *ἐν ἀρχῇ* — *in the beginning* — is, word for word, how Genesis opens, and John's first readers would have heard it at once. What is far less often noticed is what he does inside the echo. He keeps the phrase and changes the verb. Genesis: *in the beginning God made the heavens and the earth* — a verb of doing, the first creative act. John: *in the beginning the Word was* — not an act at all, but a state. At precisely the point where Genesis records something God did, John records something that was already the case. A deed at the beginning, in the one; in the other, a Someone who simply is at the beginning.

That single substitution reaches back and changes the phrase the two clauses share. In Genesis, *the beginning* is the first moment of everything that comes to be. John keeps that beginning — and then says the Word does not start there. So the beginning stops functioning as a moment the Word occupies, as though he were the earliest thing on the timeline, and functions instead as the horizon at which everything else commences, and before which he already stands. Our research concludes that this is the force of the clause, and that the echo and the departure are one gesture rather than two: John did not allude to Genesis and then add a correction. He quoted it in order to change one word in it.

## Pedagogical task

The job of this slide is to turn something your hearers already know into something that surprises them — so do not spend your energy establishing the echo. They have it. Project the slide and read the two lines aloud, one after the other, without commentary, and let the room find the changed word on its own; the pairing does that work if you let the silence after it stand. Then name what changed: a verb of doing, and a verb of being. The restraint: don't put the Greek on the screen and don't teach the tense — the imperfect has its own weight and its own occasion — and resist finishing the thought toward the deity of the Word, which this clause has not yet claimed. One sentence is the whole move: *John quoted Genesis in order to change one word in it, and the word he changed was the verb.*
