John 1:1 is three clauses long, and most of us have always heard it as a list: the Word existed, the Word was with God, the Word was God — three true things, said one after another. A list has no order that matters. This sentence does. Its second clause says *the Word was with God*, and the preposition John chose there — πρὸς τὸν θεόν — is not the ordinary word for standing beside someone; it is a word of orientation, a person turned toward another person in living fellowship. So by the time the third clause arrives, the sentence has already put two on the table: the Word, and the God he is turned toward. And that is the hinge. You cannot be in communion with yourself. When the third clause finally predicates deity — *and the Word was God* — that deity lands on someone the verse has already marked out as a distinct person, and it therefore cannot mean that the Word simply *is* the Father under a second name, because the clause before it has just said the Word is *with* the Father.

Which is why the order cannot be reversed. John does not write *the Word was God, and the Word was with God*. He writes *with God* first and *God* second, and our research concludes that the sequence is itself the argument: the personal distinction is set down before the deity is ever spoken, so that the deity, when it comes, has nowhere to collapse to. Read the clauses in the other order and each is still true, and the sentence has lost its guard — the deity arrives first, with no distinction yet in place for it to land on, and the *with* that follows sounds like an afterthought rather than the thing that keeps the verse honest. John's precision is not in any one clause. It is in the order he put them in.

## Pedagogical task

The job of this slide is to make your hearers notice something they have listened past for years — not a word in the verse but the sequence of its clauses. So do not begin by explaining the order; they will hear an explanation as a technicality. Begin with the test. Read John 1:1 aloud as John wrote it, then read it again with the last two clauses swapped — *and the Word was God, and the Word was with God* — and stop. Let the silence do the work; a room will feel the second version go slack before anyone can say why. Then project the slide and give them the reason in seven words. The restraint: do not name the ancient errors — the words for them are a second lesson, and this one lands without them — do not teach the Greek preposition from the front, and do not push on to the doctrine of the Trinity, which this verse founds but does not yet state. One sentence is the whole move: *the clauses come in an order, and the order is the argument.*
