Read John 1:12–13 together. *To all who received him, to those believing in his name, he gave the right to become children of God — who were begotten not of bloods, nor of the will of the flesh, nor of the will of a husband, but of God.* Most of us hear "eternal life" as a promise for later: something that begins when this life ends.

**The begetting is already done.** Those who received him were begotten of God: one completed act, coincident with the reception. And what it begins is a life. Ridderbos names it: "a totally new mode of existence … which as 'eternal life' has already been initiated by the work of Christ".

**It is the one life, continued.** At verses 4 and 5 the Prologue traced one divine life: the life in the Word, become the light of humanity, shining into the dark, and not overcome. Here the arc runs on. The life the first movement called ζωή, John will call ζωὴ αἰώνιος, eternal life, and it is here already begun. The begetting is the means by which that life is begun.

**And the arc does not close here.** Its nearer end is verse 14, where the Word becomes flesh. That verse belongs to the Prologue's next movement, and nothing here asserts it.

**Our research holds the grammar of the begetting, one completed act coincident with the reception, with very high confidence.** The arc that gathers it is the commentary's theological illumination, not a load-bearing claim on any verse.

*For the teacher, the shape of it in three steps.* First, **ask the room when eternal life begins.** Most will say: after death. Second, **read verses 12 and 13 and show the begetting as done**: they were begotten of God. Third, **put the slide up.** Eternal life, already begun. The one life, continued, and begun in the begetting.

*What this page does not do, said plainly.* It does not describe the consummation, or weigh the *already* against what is still to come; the commentary names where the arc is going and leaves it there. It does not take up verse 14, which belongs to the Prologue's next movement. And it does not argue how God's begetting meets the believing; the commentary on verse 13, and the slide "The Word gives, and God begets", take that up.

## Pedagogical task

This slide's job is to **let the room hear eternal life as already begun.** Most readers carry "eternal life" forward to the end of this life. **Begin with the question of when it starts**, so that the room finds the answer in verse 13's completed act. **Then let Ridderbos name it**: a new mode of existence, which as eternal life has already been initiated. **Land on the gold line**, and then on the body line as the means: the life the Prologue has been tracing since its first verses runs on into the believer, and the begetting is where it begins. **Two downloads, two jobs.** The **16:9 carries the whole move** — the headline, the gold line and the body line — and it is the one to teach from. The **9:16 carries the title and the reference alone**, for a newsletter or a bulletin column with your own writing beneath it.
