Read John 1:6–13 once more and watch nothing but the verbs. A man came. He came to bear witness. The light was in the world. The world did not know him. He came to his own, and his own did not welcome him. As many as received him were given a right. They were begotten of God. **Every one of those is finished** — told, closed, behind us. Eight verses of completed action.

**Except one phrase.** At the end of the turn, where the passage says the right is given *to those who believe in his name*, the verb stops being past. τοῖς πιστεύουσιν (*to those who believe*) is a present, and it is the only place in these eight verses where the passage leaves off narrating and faces the reader.

Look at where it sits, because the placement is the teaching. It is not at the light's shining. It is not at the refusal, which is where a preacher's instinct would put it and where the passage's weight seems to fall. **It is at the reception, and nowhere else.** The evangelist tells you about an advent that is already complete, leaves exactly one thing open, and the one thing he leaves open is the door you may walk through.

**Our research concludes that this placement is composed rather than incidental**, and the ground is the tense itself. A passage that meant to leave several doors open would not have told everything else as done. The single present is conspicuous precisely because it stands alone in a movement narrated wholly in retrospect — the coming finished, the refusal finished, even the begetting finished. That is held with high confidence, and it rests on the shape of the passage rather than on any one reader's judgement about it.

**There is a refinement, and it belongs to the teaching rather than qualifying it.** This movement is told from the far side of a completed advent, which is what makes the one present visible at all. In the Prologue's first movement the balance runs the other way — the light *shines*, in the present, and the summary is the completed thing. **The slide does not carry this and a teacher does not need it.** It is here because it is why the reading holds: the open phrase is not an accident of Greek but the one exception in a passage built out of finished things.

*For the teacher, the shape of it in three steps.* First, **read the passage aloud and have them count the finished verbs** — came, was, knew not, received, were begotten. Doing it out loud is most of the work, because the pattern is obvious once heard and invisible on the page. Second, **stop at "to those who believe" and name what changed.** Not the meaning; the tense. Third, **ask where it sits** — not at the shining, not at the refusal, but at the receiving. That is the part they will still have on Tuesday.

*What this page does not do, said plainly.* It does not exegete verse 12, or take up what the right amounts to, or what it means to become a child of God — each has its own treatment in the commentary. It does not argue what the begetting of verse 13 is. It does not claim that the believing is the passage's achievement; the passage keeps God's act for its last word, which is a different point and belongs with it. And **it does not claim the open phrase is hidden.** It is in every translation. The claim is that its placement is deliberate, and that a reader who notices where it falls has understood something about how the passage was built.

## Pedagogical task

This slide's job is to **move one phrase from the middle of a verse to the centre of attention**, not to teach a doctrine of faith. Your people are not confused about "to those who believe"; they have simply never been shown that it is the only thing in the passage still open. So project it and do the one thing it licenses: read the verbs, let them hear that all of them are done, and then stop at the one that is not. **Do not preach the believing from the front.** The moment this becomes an appeal, the observation is spent and the passage's own restraint is lost — and the restraint is the point. Do not take up what being born of God means; that is a study of its own and it will swallow the time. And **do not oversell it**: this is not a recovered reading. It is the ordinary shape of the passage, seen. **Two downloads, two jobs.** The **16:9 carries the whole move** — the claim, the phrase from the verse, and the reason — and it is the one to project while you teach from it. The **9:16 carries the title and the reference alone**, which makes it the one for a newsletter or a bulletin column with your own writing beneath it.
