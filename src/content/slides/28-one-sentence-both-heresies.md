John 1:1 has three clauses: *In the beginning was the Word* · *and the Word was with God* · *and the Word was God.* The first sets the time — before anything was made, the Word already was. The second and third are the two that guard each other, and each guards against a different error.

**Take away *the Word was with God*.** What is left reads *In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was God* — and on its own that can be heard as saying the Word simply *is* God, with no distinction between them at all: one person, going by a second name. The church met that teaching in the late second and early third centuries, in Noetus, Praxeas and above all Sabellius, and came to call it **modalism**. Its claim is that Father, Son and Spirit are not three who are eternally distinct but three modes, masks or roles that the one God takes up in turn — Father in creation, Son in redemption, Spirit in the church. Tertullian wrote a whole book against Praxeas over it, and the charge he pressed was the one the teaching cannot escape: if the Son just is the Father, then it was the Father who was born and the Father who suffered on the cross. John's second clause shuts the door before it opens. *With* is a word of company, and nothing is ever with itself.

**Take away *the Word was God*.** What is left reads *In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God* — and on its own that can be heard as saying the Word is a companion of God but not himself God: an exalted being, perhaps the first and greatest thing God ever made, but standing on the creature's side of the line between God and everything else. The church met that in the early fourth century in Arius, who taught that there was a time when the Son was not, and condemned it at Nicaea in 325. **Subordinationism** is the name it goes by. The point needs one piece of care: Scripture does speak of an order between the Father and the Son — the Son is sent, the Son obeys, the Son is the one *through* whom the Father makes all things — and that ordered relation is not the error. The error is making the Son *less in being*, a lesser god or a made thing. John's third clause shuts that door too, and it does it without a comparative: not *like* God, not *divine* in some lower sense, but *was God*.

**So neither clause can be spared**, because each rules out exactly what the other, standing alone, would let in. Drop the distinction and the deity becomes modalism; drop the deity and the distinction becomes subordinationism. While both stand, neither error can take hold — two stones in an arch, each leaning on the other, so that neither can fall.

**One thing this page is not saying.** John was not writing against modalism or against Arius. Neither existed when he wrote; the councils that named them were two and three centuries away. What he wrote was a sentence whose parts hold each other, and the church later found that the errors it had to reject were already excluded by it. The precision is not ours, read back into John. It is John's.

*For the preacher, the shape of it in three steps.* First, **do the removal out loud** — read verse 1 with the second clause taken away, then with the third taken away, and let the congregation hear each maimed version before you name anything. The demonstration has to come before the vocabulary or the vocabulary is all anyone remembers. Second, **give each error one sentence and no more**: the Father under a second name; a lesser god on the creature's side. Third, **put the verse back together whole** and stop on it.

*What this page does not do, said plainly.* It does not exegete the preposition in *with God*, or take up the missing article at *the Word was God* — both have their own slides. It does not trace the councils, quote the creed, or argue the history of either error beyond naming where it arose. It does not develop what makes the persons distinct, or enter the vocabulary the early church coined for them. **It removes one clause at a time and shows what follows, and stops there.**

## Pedagogical task

This slide's job is to let a teacher **show a congregation that John's precision is John's own** — not a fourth-century framework read back into a first-century sentence. The restraint is that you must not turn it into a lesson on the history of heresy: two names, one sentence each, and back to the verse. A congregation that leaves able to define modalism but unable to say why the second clause matters has been given the wrong thing. Project it and work it in the verse's own order — remove, hear, name, restore. **The removal is the whole teaching move**, and it only works aloud: a hearer who merely sees the words *modalism* and *subordinationism* on a screen has learned two labels, while a hearer who has just heard verse 1 read with a clause missing has felt the gap the label names. From the pulpit the whole thing is one sentence: *take either clause away and the other, left alone, becomes an error the church had to condemn.* **What not to do beyond that:** do not let it become a quiz on the terms, and do not suggest John was answering these errors — he was not; they came later, and the page says so in terms. **Two downloads, two jobs.** The **wide slide carries the whole move** — the count, the two denials and the reason — and it is the one to project while you teach from it. The **tall slide carries the count and the gold line without the denials**, which makes it the one for a bulletin, a newsletter column or an invitation: it poses the puzzle and sends the reader to find out how it is answered.
