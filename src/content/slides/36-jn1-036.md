Read John 1:12 in the Authorized Version: *But as many as received him, to them gave he power to become the sons of God.* Most of us hear "power" as something put into a person: a strength, a capacity, an inner ability to become what we could not be before. Many sermons have been preached on that power.

**It is not a power. It is a right.** John's word is ἐξουσία. It comes from ἔξεστιν, *it is permissible*: what is permitted, authorized, rightfully yours. Bultmann surveyed the word across the Gospel and found that "Ἐξουσία in John is always used in the normal Greek sense of 'right', 'authority'". Dodd went further: the Authorized Version's "power," which makes the word indistinguishable from δύναμις, is one of its most misleading mistranslations.

**Two readers reached the same word four centuries apart.** Calvin, in the sixteenth century: ἐξουσία "is sometimes put for ἀξίωσις, (*a claim*,) a meaning which falls in admirably with this passage." Barth, in the twentieth: "not possibility in the sense of dynamis, power, might, strength, but in the sense of axiōsis, authority, legitimation, right." Neither was following the other. Westcott says the same in his own words: the word "does not describe mere ability, but legitimate, rightful authority, derived from a competent source which includes the idea of power."

**Why it matters.** A power put into them would make the believers' standing something they carry and must keep up. A right given to them makes it something conferred, by the one who had it to give. The verse names them by what they did — they received him — and then tells us what was *given* to them. The standing is his gift, not their achievement.

**Not every careful reader agrees.** Schnackenburg thinks "to be entitled to" is "too weak," and McHugh reads the word as "the possibility, the ability, a capability, and therefore the potential, the power 'to become children of God'." Both want to guard something true: no one becomes a child of God by nature or by their own strength. The commentary agrees with what they deny, and answers them on the word.

**Our research concludes that John's ἐξουσία at 1:12 is a right, not a power infused.** The lexical sense is held with very high confidence.

*For the teacher, the shape of it in three steps.* First, **read the verse in the Authorized Version, and ask the room what "power" means there.** Let them say it: strength, ability, something inside. Second, **read the Greek word's meaning and Bultmann's survey, and then put the headline on the screen.** Third, **read Calvin and Barth side by side, four hundred years apart, and then the gold line.** A right, not a power. Given to them, not put into them.

*What this page does not do, said plainly.* It does not take up how the right works — whether a right granted is already the standing it names, and what the church confesses about the believer's standing on the strength of it; that is a later page's. It does not take up why John says "children" and not "sons." It does not take up what it means to believe in his name. And it does not explain how anyone *becomes* a child of God; that is the next verse's.

## Pedagogical task

This slide's job is to **take one word back from a familiar mistranslation**. Most rooms that have heard "power to become the sons of God" hear an inner strength. **Lead with the Authorized Version, not the Greek**: let the room say what "power" means before anyone corrects it. **Let Calvin and Barth carry the weight**, two readers four centuries apart reaching the same word without borrowing it from each other. **Be fair to the other side.** Schnackenburg and McHugh keep "power" for a good reason, and the point is not that believers are left without God's help. The point is that what they received was *given*. **Stop at the word.** How a right given becomes a standing held, and what the church confesses about it, is a later lesson; do not let this one swallow it. **Two downloads, two jobs.** The **16:9 carries the whole move** — the headline, the gold line and the verse beneath it — and it is the one to teach from. The **9:16 carries the title and the reference alone**, for a newsletter or a bulletin column with your own writing beneath it.
