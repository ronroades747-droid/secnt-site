Read John 1:12–13 together. *To all who received him, to those believing in his name, he gave the right to become children of God — who were begotten not of bloods, nor of the will of the flesh, nor of the will of a husband, but of God.* Most of us hear one act in those two verses, and one agent: God, or Christ, doing a single thing for the believer.

**The text names two.** In verse 12, the Word gives the right: *he gave*. In verse 13, God begets: *they were begotten of God*. The distribution of agency is the text's own, and the text keeps the two distinct.

**And there is an order between them.** The giving issues from the begetting. Barth states the order: "As the proclamation of a right it issues from existent divine power and its exercise. The Word summons them to be what they already are from God".

**The God who begets is the God of the Prologue's opening verses**, the God with whom the Word was in the beginning, and not the Word. But the Prologue does not yet call him the believers' Father. That title waits for 20:17, and it is not anticipated here.

**Our research holds the two agents with high confidence, at each of their parts.** The grammar of the begetting is held with very high confidence.

*For the teacher, the shape of it in three steps.* First, **read verses 12 and 13 together and ask the room who acts in them.** Most will name one. Second, **show the two verbs**: the Word gave the right; they were begotten of God. Third, **put the slide up.** The Word gives, and God begets. Two agents, one order. And let the room notice that the God who begets is not yet called Father.

*What this page does not do, said plainly.* It does not argue what the begetting is, or what it is not; the commentary on verse 13, and the slide "Not something the world can produce", take those up. It does not take up how God's act meets the believing that receives him; the commentary holds that God's act comes first as the ground, and that John does not say how it meets faith. And it does not settle the distribution across Father, Son and Spirit, which the commentary sets out as illumination and leaves as it is set out.

## Pedagogical task

This slide's job is to **let the room hear two agents where it expects one.** Most readers run verses 12 and 13 together as a single act of grace. **Begin with the question of who acts**, so that the room finds the two verbs for itself. **Then let Barth state the order**: the right is proclaimed from a power already at work, and the Word summons the children to be what they already are from God. **Land on the gold line**, and then on the body line as the turn the Prologue holds back: the children are begotten of God, and the Gospel takes its time before it gives them the name of their Father. **Two downloads, two jobs.** The **16:9 carries the whole move** — the headline, the gold line and the held-back title — and it is the one to teach from. The **9:16 carries the title and the reference alone**, for a newsletter or a bulletin column with your own writing beneath it.
