John's first verse makes three statements about the Word: he was in the beginning (ἐν ἀρχῇ); he was with God (πρὸς τὸν θεόν); and he was God (θεὸς ἦν ὁ λόγος). The second verse — *This one was in the beginning with God* — sounds like a repetition of it, and is often taught as one: the opening said again, for emphasis. Set the two verses side by side and that reading dissolves. Verse 2 keeps two of the three — the beginning, and the being-with-God — and withholds the third: the climactic clause, the very line everyone remembers. Our research concludes the selection is deliberate. A restatement would repeat all three clauses, or repeat at random; verse 2 does neither — it keeps exactly two and drops the climax, and a selection is a choice, not an echo. And the choice takes nothing back: *the Word was God* was established one verse earlier and stands; verse 2 withholds the line from repetition without withdrawing a word of it. The verse readers pass over as an echo turns out to be curated — the first clue that John is doing something in verse 2, and an open door into reading the whole passage closely.

## Pedagogical task

This slide's job is to make an absence visible. Project it when you teach John 1:1–2 and let the class do the discovering: the headline names what happened, and the gold line is the evidence — verse 2 itself, in full. Read verse 1 aloud first, all three statements. Then point to the gold line and ask them to find what went missing. Give them the silence; the absence hides in plain sight, and finding it themselves is what makes it land. Then hand them the inference at the bottom of the slide: an echo does not choose — a selection is a choice. The restraint: resist answering *why* from the front. The question, left standing, is what carries a class into verse 2 as a verse worth reading closely, and the fuller account belongs to a later lesson. Make only the one clarification that keeps the surprise honest — nothing was taken back; the famous line was established one verse earlier and did not need saying twice. One sentence is the whole move: *put the verses side by side, find the missing line, and ask why a mere repetition would drop it.*
