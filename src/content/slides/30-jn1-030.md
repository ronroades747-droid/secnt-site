There is a sentence in John's prologue that most readers step over on the way to something else. *He was not the light, but came to bear witness about the light.* **The ear takes it as a correction of somebody's enthusiasm** — John the Baptist put gently back in his place, told not to get ideas about himself. Read that way it is a footnote: a parenthesis inside a parenthesis, clearing up a misunderstanding nobody in the room was having. **What it is instead is the place where the passage turns.**

Look at where the sentence sits rather than at what it denies. Everything from verse 6 forward has been about a man — he came, he was sent from God, his name was John, he came for a witness. Everything from verse 9 forward is about the light — it was the true light, it was coming into the world, the world did not know it. **The handover happens inside verse 8**, and it happens in one breath: the clause οὐκ ἦν ἐκεῖνος τὸ φῶς (*he was not the light*) closes the account of John, and the clause that follows it — ἀλλ᾽ ἵνα μαρτυρήσῃ περὶ τοῦ φωτός (*but that he might bear witness concerning the light*) — turns the sentence outward and names what the rest of the passage will be about.

**Two ways of reading the unit meet at that verse, and they were not asking the same question.** The commentators who divide the passage close the account of John at verse 8 — Barrett, Lincoln and McHugh each mark the joint there, working from the flow of the argument and the shape of the sentences. The readers who parse the unit's verbs, Harris among them, find its long interior opening at the same verse. Neither group was consulted about the other's question, and they land on the same clause. **Our research concludes that verse 8 is the hinge of the movement**, and that the handing-off is what the sentence is for.

**There is a refinement here, and it belongs to the teaching rather than qualifying it.** The two joints do not fall in precisely the same place. οὐκ ἦν ἐκεῖνος τὸ φῶς does double duty — it is the last thing said about John and the first verb of the stretch that follows — while the clause after it lies between the two joints and measures the distance between them. The witness is handed to the light inside a single sentence. **The slide does not carry this and a teacher does not need it to teach the verse.** It is here because it is why the reading holds.

*For the teacher, the shape of it in three steps.* First, **say the misreading out loud before you correct it** — that verse 8 sounds like John being taken down a peg. Naming it is most of the work, because nobody defends a reading they have heard said aloud. Second, **read verses 6 to 9 straight through and ask who the subject is.** The answer changes inside verse 8, and your people can hear it change without being told. Third, **give them the sentence and stop**: John stops being the subject, and the light takes over. That is the part they will still have on Tuesday.

*What this page does not do, said plainly.* It does not argue where the prologue's second movement begins or ends; it rests on bounds the commentary establishes elsewhere. It does not take up who John the Baptist was, what his witness accomplished, or how this Gospel uses the word *witness*. It does not exegete verse 9 — the true light, the illumining, the coming into the world. And **it does not claim that verse 8 stops being a denial.** It is one. The claim is that the denial is doing structural work, and that the work is the more important half.

## Pedagogical task

This slide's job is to **move one verse from the margin to the hinge**, not to teach a doctrine of witness. Your people are not confused about verse 8; they have never been given a reason to slow down at it. So project it and do the one thing it licenses: read the verse, ask who the subject is on either side of it, and let them hear the change. Then stop. **Do not parse the Greek from the front.** The clause boundaries are the reason this is true; they are not the lesson, and the moment you start explaining them you have made this an hour about grammar. Do not take up the Baptist's ministry — that is a study of its own and it will swallow the time. And **do not oversell it**: this is not a hidden meaning or a recovered reading. It is the ordinary shape of the passage, seen. **Two downloads, two jobs.** The **16:9 carries the whole move** — the claim, the phrase from the verse, and the reason — and it is the one to project while you teach from it. The **9:16 carries the title and the reference alone**, which makes it the one for a newsletter or a bulletin column with your own writing beneath it.
