There is a word in the first verse of John that your English Bible has no way to show you, and it is the smallest word there is. Twice in one sentence John writes the Greek noun for God. In the second clause he writes it with the definite article — πρὸς τὸν θεόν, *the* God, the one the Word is with. In the third he writes it with no article at all — θεὸς ἦν ὁ λόγος, God, bare. Three words apart, the article goes in and then it is left out; and in English both clauses come through the same way, *the Word was with God*, *the Word was God*. The same word twice, and nothing on the surface to see.

Our research reads the variation as deliberate, and as the formal mark by which the verse does two different things in two consecutive clauses. Where the verse needs to keep the persons distinct it uses the article, which points: *the* God, that one, the person the Word is with. Where it needs to assert the shared nature it drops the article, so that what is said is not that the Word is that same person but that the Word has that same nature. The counterfactual is what shows the choice was made rather than fallen into: had John written the article into the third clause as well, he would have identified the Word with the very person he had just said the Word was with, and the verse would have collapsed into itself. He did not. Rudolf Schnackenburg is the commentator who taught the field to read the variation, observing that the article-less *theos* of the third clause does not simply identify the Word with the God named just before, but predicates the nature the two hold in common.

*One thing worth naming before you teach it, because a sharp listener will find it: both lines on the slide contain an article — the ὁ before λόγος, once in each. It is not the one under discussion. It belongs to* the Word*, not to* God*, and the claim is about the noun for God in each clause: with the article in the one, bare in the other. That the Word keeps its own article in both lines is in fact the point — it is what leaves the article on* God *as the only thing that changes. Say so before someone asks, and it lands cleanly.*

*Schnackenburg's observation is at* The Gospel according to St John, *vol. 1, translated by Kevin Smyth (New York: Herder and Herder, 1968), page 234, in the course of his treatment of John 1:1c. The Greek on the slide is the critical text of the verse, each clause set without its coordinating* καί*; the English renderings above are this commentary's own, and the slide itself carries no English beyond its headline.*

## Pedagogical task

This slide's job is to make one word visible, and nothing else. Everything the point depends on is the presence of three letters in one line and their absence in the next, which is invisible in English and easy to read past in Greek — so the slide holds the two clauses still, one above the other, and marks the article in gold so the eye cannot miss it. Project it and give them a moment before you say anything; the frame is built to be looked at rather than read. Then do the one thing it licenses: point at the gold word, then point at the line below it where there is no gold word, and say what each does — where the article is there it names *who*, and where it is gone it names *what*. From the pulpit that is the whole move and it takes ten seconds. In a classroom, use the same frame differently: put a thumb over the one gold word, ask the room what is different between the two lines, and let someone find it. The slide carries no English, so the discovery is a real one and not a reading exercise — that is worth more than the telling, and it is available here in a way it rarely is. The restraint is the discipline. Do not parse the clauses from the front, do not teach the Greek article as a topic, and above all do not turn this into the argument about whether the missing article makes the Word *a god* — that is a different question with a different answer, and raising it here trades a thing your people can see for a controversy they cannot settle. What is in front of them is one word, present and then withheld, doing two jobs. Leave it at that and it will stay with them.
