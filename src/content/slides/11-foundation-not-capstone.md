Almost everyone reads John 1:1 as a climb. *In the beginning was the Word. And the Word was with God. And the Word was God.* Three clauses, each one higher, and the third is where the sentence arrives — the line that settles who Jesus is. Read that way, the last clause is a capstone: the stone set on top of a building that is already standing. Murray Harris, who wrote a full-length study of the New Testament passages that call Jesus God, turns that architecture over. The third clause is not a capstone laid on the first two after they are built; it is the basis on which they rest — the ground on which the first two can be said to be true at all. Our research follows him here, with the weight of the major commentators behind the reading. Ask why John can say the Word was there in the beginning, uncreated and without a starting point of his own: only because the Word shares, inherently, in the very nature of God. Ask why John can say the Word was with God in unbroken fellowship with the Father: the same reason. Take away the deity, and the eternity and the fellowship have nothing to stand on. Notice what this does *not* do. It does not cancel the climax — the third clause really is the height the sentence reaches, and Harris's point sits beside that one rather than replacing it. The clause is both at once: the top of the climb, and the rock under it.

## Pedagogical task

This slide's job is to reverse a direction of reading, not to add a doctrine. Project it when you reach the third clause, and then do the one thing it licenses: preach the verse backwards. Start at *and the Word was God*, and walk back up — this is why he could be with God, and this is why he was there in the beginning. Your people have heard the staircase all their lives; let them hear what the staircase is standing on. The restraint: don't argue the grammar from the front, and don't reach for the Greek — the move is architectural, and the moment you make it a language lesson it stops being something they can carry home. And don't overturn the climax to win the point; say *both*, and the slide's own gloss will hold the pair for you. One sentence is the whole move: *preach the verse backwards once, starting from the third clause.*
