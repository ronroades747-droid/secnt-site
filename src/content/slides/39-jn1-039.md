Read John 1:10 slowly. *He was in the world, and the world came to be through him, and the world did not know him.* Then put two other sayings beside it. *God so loved the world that he gave his only Son* (3:16). And to the disciples: *if the world hates you, know that it hated me first* (15:18). Most of us hear two different worlds in those verses. There is a world God loves, and there is a world that hates him and his people, and we sort each verse into one or the other.

**One world, three things said of it.** In verse 10, "the world" means the same thing in all three clauses: the inhabited human world. What changes is not the world but what is said of it. It is the place of his presence. It is his own handiwork. It is the one that refuses him.

**John McHugh reads the Gospel's whole use of the word from that verse.** He calls its threefold use there "certainly a description, almost a definition, of the sense in which John is going to use the term." And he concludes that in the Fourth Gospel the world "denotes the divinely created world which has, as Genesis teaches, rejected God, but which is at the same time not beyond salvation."

**On that reading, it is one world all the way through.** The world that hates the disciples (15:18–19), the world the Spirit convicts concerning judgment (16:8–11), the world God so loved (3:16), and the world whose sin the Lamb of God takes away (1:29) are not four worlds. They are one. Hated, judged, loved, redeemed: four things said of the same world, under what it does and under what is done for it.

**Our research concludes that "the world" names one world across verse 10, held with very high confidence.** Reading the Gospel's later uses of the word from it is McHugh's reading, and this commentary takes it on his reading, each later verse read in its own place.

*For the teacher, the shape of it in three steps.* First, **read 3:16 and 15:18 aloud, one after the other, and ask the room whether these are the same world.** Most will say no. Second, **read verse 10, and let them hear the three things said of one world** — present in it, made it, refused by it. Third, **put the slide up.** One world, not two. Hated and loved: the same world.

*What this page does not do, said plainly.* It does not argue what "the world" means clause by clause in verse 10, or why the refusal was a refusal of its Maker; the commentary on verse 10, and the slide "The Maker refused", take those up. It does not settle the meaning of each later verse; it gives McHugh's reading of them, which the commentary follows. It does not take up verse 11's "his own", which is the next page's. And it does not say that the world's hatred is not real. It is; the claim is that it is the same world God loved.

## Pedagogical task

This slide's job is to **stop the room dividing John's "world" in two.** Most readers file "God so loved the world" and "the world hates you" under different worlds, and lose what the Gospel is saying by it. **Begin with the two verses side by side**, so the problem is the room's own before the slide answers it. **Then go to verse 10**, where one world is seen three ways, and **give the wider reading as McHugh's**, as the commentary does. **Land on the gold line.** The world that hates is not some other world than the one God loved; it is the same world, and that is why the Son is given to it. **Two downloads, two jobs.** The **16:9 carries the whole move** — the headline, the gold line and the four things said of the world — and it is the one to teach from. The **9:16 carries the title and the reference alone**, for a newsletter or a bulletin column with your own writing beneath it.
