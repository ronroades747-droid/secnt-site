*The Word was with God.* Almost everyone hears that as a statement about position — God here, the Word beside him, two figures in the same room before anything else exists. The English preposition invites the picture and the picture is hard to shake. But the sentence is not built out of the ordinary Greek for being near somebody. John wrote πρὸς τὸν θεόν, and the exact construction he used — a person eternally being πρός another person — has, so far as the evidence goes, no precedent in classical Greek and no instance anywhere in the Greek Old Testament for merely being near. That matters, and it is worth being careful about what it does and does not settle. The preposition's own range had widened by the first century and could carry simple position; our research concedes that without flinching, and it is why the reading has to be earned rather than assumed. What the evidence will not supply is an instance of *this* idiom used to say *beside*. John chose a construction that, in the Greek available to him, was not the construction of position — and our research reads the clause, with the weight of the major commentators behind it, as active personal communion: two persons in living relationship before there was anything else at all. That reading is load-bearing rather than ornamental. It is what makes the Word a person, genuinely distinct from the God he is with — and if the clause is read flat, as scene-setting, the verse loses something it cannot afford to lose.

## Pedagogical task

This slide's job is to swap one picture for another, not to teach a paradigm. Project it at verse 1 and do the one thing it licenses: say plainly that *with* here is not the word for standing beside somebody, it is the word for being in relationship with somebody — and then let the Greek on the screen be the evidence rather than the lesson. Your hearers do not need to read it or say it; they need to see that there is a specific word there and that it was chosen. The move is a substitution, and it lands in one sentence: *communion, not geography*. The restraint: do not parse, do not translate the phrase word by word from the front, and do not press the point into a claim about direction or facing — the ground that holds firm is the fellowship, and the more specific pictures are a further step this slide is not taking. In a classroom the same material wants the evidence laid out at length, and that is what the lecture is for; the link is on this page.
