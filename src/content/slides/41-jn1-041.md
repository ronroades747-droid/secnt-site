Read John 1:12 slowly. *To all who received him, to those believing in his name, he gave the right to become children of God.* Most of us hear "right" in one of two ways. Either it is a power put into us, a capacity we must now use. Or it is a permission held in suspense, a door left open that we may or may not walk through.

**It is neither.** What the receivers are given is a standing granted by a competent authority, whose pronouncement constitutes the standing it declares. The right is conferred by declaration, effectual because of who declares it.

**Two readers say so in their own words.** Westcott: not "mere ability, but legitimate, rightful authority, derived from a competent source which includes the idea of power." And Calvin, long before him: the Evangelist does not mean "any intermediate faculty, or one which does not include the full and complete effect".

**It is a right *to become*.** Westcott puts the whole sequence in a sentence: "The fruit is not given at once, but the seed." What is given is a beginning, and the grant makes real what it names.

**Our research concludes that the right carries its effect, held with high confidence.** The objection that the word names a real capacity conferred, and that a bare entitlement is "too weak" (Schnackenburg), is a serious one, and the reading is held knowing its weight. That the word means a right is held with very high confidence.

*For the teacher, the shape of it in three steps.* First, **read the verse and ask the room what kind of "right" this is**: a strength put into them, or a permission they may use. Let both answers be heard. Second, **read Westcott and Calvin**, and let the room hear a right that is granted and effectual at once. Third, **put the slide up.** A right that carries its effect. The grant makes it real — never by feeling or effort.

*What this page does not do, said plainly.* It does not argue the word itself, a right and not a power; the slide "A right, not a power" takes that up. It does not take up why John says "children" and not "sons", or what it means to believe in his name. It does not explain how anyone *becomes* a child of God; that is the next verse's. And it does not say that believing certifies itself. The commentary holds that the standing is for those who go on receiving.

## Pedagogical task

This slide's job is to **take the room past the word to what the word does.** "A right, not a power" settled the word; this slide settles what kind of right it is. Most rooms will hear either an inner strength or an open permission. **Name both, and let the verse refuse both.** Then **let Westcott and Calvin carry the weight**: a right that is rightful authority, and one that includes the full and complete effect. **Land on the gold line, and on the body line as the pastoral word**: the child of God is not made by feeling, or by effort, but by a grant. **Be fair to the other side.** Schnackenburg's objection is real, and the commentary holds the reading at high, not very high. **Two downloads, two jobs.** The **16:9 carries the whole move** — the headline, the gold line and the pastoral line — and it is the one to teach from. The **9:16 carries the title and the reference alone**, for a newsletter or a bulletin column with your own writing beneath it.
