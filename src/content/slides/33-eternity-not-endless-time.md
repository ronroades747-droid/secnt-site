Ask almost anyone what eternity is and you will be handed a picture of duration: **a line of time stretched out endlessly in both directions**, time that never started and never stops. It is the natural picture, and the classical doctrine is both *more* than that and *other* than that. In Herman Bavinck's articulation **eternity excludes three things together — a beginning, an end, and the succession of moments** — and it is the third that carries the weight. The first two a hearer expects; they are what *endless* already means. **The third says there is no before and after in God's being at all**: he does not pass from one moment to the next, accumulating a past and approaching a future, and there is no was-then, is-now, will-be in him. Succession has no purchase on his being. Our research reads this as **the doctrinal form of the imperfect** that opens John's Gospel: *in the beginning was* — ἦν — is not a verb that starts a clock but one that forecloses one, foreclosing a beginning and succession within the being at a single stroke. **And Bavinck does something striking with this verse.** Among the texts by which he shows that God existed before the world was, **he lists John 1:1 itself** — the Prologue's own opening clause taken up into the dogmatic tradition as a proof of the very perfection it grounds. **The eternal I AM: being, not becoming.**

*For the teacher, the shape of it in three steps.* First, **let them draw the line** — ask what eternity is and accept the timeline, because the correction only lands on a picture that has been said out loud. Second, **give the three exclusions in order**, and slow down on the third; the first two are what your hearers already meant by the word, and the third is the one they have never been handed. Third, **take the line away rather than lengthening it.** What is left is not a very long life. It is being, and not becoming.

*What this page does not do, said plainly.* It does not take up the further question of whether God's being depends on anything outside himself — the tradition's word for the answer is **aseity**, from the Latin *a se*, from himself — nor the care that question then requires, that underived being is not the same thing as being one's own cause. It does not treat the asymmetry between the one who makes and everything made, or exegete the imperfect ἦν as grammar — that is its own slide. It does not take up the objections a doctrine at this height attracts. **It gives one exclusion, and the verse the tradition hangs it on, and stops there.**

## Pedagogical task

This slide's job is to **replace a picture, not to add a fact**. Your hearers are not missing information about eternity; they are holding a diagram of it, and the diagram is what has to go. So project it and resist the two temptations. **Do not stretch the timeline** — saying *and it goes on forever in both directions* is agreeing with the picture in a louder voice, and it is the failure this slide exists to prevent. **And do not explain the Greek from the front.** The gold word is there to be pointed at, not parsed; the anchor's job is to keep the claim tethered to the verse rather than floating off into the doctrine of God in general. One sentence is the whole move: *eternity is not a longer line, it is no line at all.* **Two downloads, two jobs.** The **wide slide carries the whole move** — the claim, the anchor and the three exclusions — and it is the one to project while you teach. The **tall slide carries the claim and the anchor without the three**, which makes it the one for a bulletin, a newsletter column or an invitation: it poses the question and sends the reader to find the answer.
