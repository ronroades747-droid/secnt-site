# The Text and Its Translation

SECNT is a commentary on the Greek text of the New Testament, not on any English version of it.

## The Greek text

The text commented on is the critical text of the modern editions, cited from the Nestle–Aland 28th edition. Where a reading is in question, or the punctuation is, each Commentary settles it in its treatment of the text, sets out the evidence, and states the confidence at which the decision is held. The Greek printed at the head of each unit is that working text.

## The English translation

The English printed with it is the commentary's own translation of that working text. It is close: it follows the wording and order of the Greek as far as English allows, and where it must choose, it prefers the rendering that shows what the Greek says over the one that reads more smoothly. Where a rendering carries a decision the exegesis makes (a disputed reading, the force of a tense, the punctuation of a verse), a note says so and links to the page where the decision is argued.

## Why not a published translation

Every published English version renders a Greek text and a punctuation its translators chose. Where this commentary decides a reading or a punctuation differently, a published version printed at the head of the passage would present one text while the commentary argued from another. Its own translation keeps the English and the argument on the same text.

## Other translations

Published versions are engaged where their renderings bear on a decision, as part of the evidence, alongside the commentators, and are quoted at the words in question.

## Scripture outside the passage

Where the commentary cites a passage it does not itself argue (an Old Testament text the evangelist echoes, or a parallel elsewhere in the New Testament), the English is the Berean Standard Bible (BSB). It is a balanced translation that keeps the original languages transparent to the reader. It was made by a committee of scholars working alongside pastors and lay readers, and its team regularly invites public feedback on the text. It has been in the public domain since 30 April 2023, so it can be quoted in full, in any medium, without permission. Where the commentary argues a passage's wording, the rendering is its own.
